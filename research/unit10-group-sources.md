# Unit 10 sources — Group Counseling

Evidence base for Unit 10 readings. Each source has a one-line authority note. Inline citations in
wiki pages reference these numbers, e.g. `[S1]`.

_Researched 2026-07-19. Tier 3 "rounding out" unit, built to the same in-depth standard as Units
1–9. It covers **why groups heal differently** than individual work: **Yalom's therapeutic factors**
(the centerpiece — group cohesiveness and interpersonal learning above all), the **stages of group
development** (Tuckman; Corey's initial→transition→working→final), the **leader's role** (the
Lieberman/Yalom/Miles four functions and the linear/curvilinear outcome findings), the **ASGW types
of group work**, **managing difficult members**, and the **ethics of group formation** (screening,
preparation, and the confidentiality limit unique to groups). Anchored on a **peer-reviewed clinical
reference** (StatPearls), the field's **professional bodies** (AGPA, ASGW, ACA, APA/Div. 49), and the
two **anchor books** (Yalom & Leszcz; Corey; plus Lieberman/Yalom/Miles). The three books are offline
and cited via reputable secondary sources + the primary publication record, flagged. Two contested
threads are carried deliberately and quizzed at evaluate level: (1) the therapeutic-factor framework
is **clinically derived, not a validated factor structure** — rankings vary by group type, member,
and study, and cohesion's efficacy evidence is thinner than its clinical centrality implies; and
(2) group work can **harm** as well as help (the encounter-group "casualty" finding), which is why
leadership balance, screening, and preparation are treated as ethical, not optional._

---

## Tier: the core clinical reference

- **[S1] Malhotra, A., & Baker, J. *Group Therapy.* StatPearls (NCBI Bookshelf), 2023.**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549812/
  _Peer-reviewed clinical reference (same series anchoring Units 2/6/9). Authority: high._
  The **AGPA-identified therapeutic factors** (altruism, catharsis, cohesiveness, corrective
  recapitulation, socializing techniques, existential factors, imitative behavior, imparting
  information, interpersonal learning [input/output], instillation of hope, self-understanding,
  universality). **Cohesion is "the core mechanism of action"** and "a **client-client equivalent to
  the client-therapist therapeutic alliance**"; it "facilitates other therapeutic factors and
  correlates positively with clinical improvement." **"Group psychotherapy is as effective as
  individual psychotherapy"** → more cost-effective, widens access. The **four leadership functions**
  (executive function, caring, emotional stimulation, meaning-attribution). **Five developmental
  stages** (forming/pre-affiliation → storming/power-and-control [the *barometric event*] →
  norming/intimacy → performing/differentiation → adjourning/separation). **Confidentiality** is a
  unique challenge because "clients play a role in treating their peers" — addressed in informed
  consent, members sign a confidentiality agreement, requirements vary by jurisdiction. Structure:
  typically **7–10 members**, **90–120 minutes**, closed- vs. open-ended, homogeneity/heterogeneity
  balance, circular seating, solo vs. co-leadership.

## Tier: Yalom — the therapeutic factors and the here-and-now

- **[S2] Yalom, I. D., & Leszcz, M. (2020). *The Theory and Practice of Group Psychotherapy* (6th ed.). Basic Books.**
  https://www.basicbooks.com/titles/irvin-d-yalom/the-theory-and-practice-of-group-psychotherapy/9781541617576/
  _The syllabus anchor text and the defining work of the field. Book, offline — every claim used is corroborated by S1/S3. Authority: very high._
  The **eleven therapeutic factors** (instillation of hope, universality, imparting information,
  altruism, corrective recapitulation of the primary family group, development of socializing
  techniques, imitative behavior, **interpersonal learning**, **group cohesiveness**, catharsis,
  existential factors). Yalom's judgment that **cohesiveness and interpersonal learning are primary**;
  cohesiveness is "the group therapy analog of the relationship in individual therapy" and the
  **precondition** for the other factors. The **social microcosm** (members recreate their habitual
  interpersonal world inside the group), the **here-and-now** (the group's most potent focus is the
  immediate present, not the "there-and-then"), the **self-reflective loop** (experience the
  here-and-now, then **illuminate the process**), and **process illumination** (commenting on the
  relationship implication of what's said, not just the content). The taxonomy of **"problem group
  members"** (the monopolist, the silent member, the boring patient, the help-rejecting complainer,
  and characterologically difficult members).

- **[S3] Psychology Town. *Core Principles of Group Psychotherapy: Yalom's Therapeutic Factors* (and companion pages on group process/dynamics and types).**
  https://psychology.town/psychotherapeutic-methods/core-principles-group-psychotherapy-yalom/
  _Educational secondary summary — **orientation-tier**, used for factor definitions and to corroborate S2's here-and-now / social-microcosm framing; cross-checked against S1/S2. Authority: moderate._
  Definitions of each of the eleven factors; **interpersonal learning** unpacked as (a) the importance
  of interpersonal relationships, (b) the **corrective emotional experience**, and (c) the group as a
  **social microcosm** where members "inadvertently enact their distress and display dysfunctional
  patterns of relating, which the therapist and other members can then reflect back." Confirms
  interpersonal learning as one Yalom regarded as **most significant** and "one that cannot be
  replicated in individual therapy."

## Tier: efficacy — group vs. individual

- **[S4] American Psychological Association. *Gary Burlingame studies outcomes and group therapy* (APA Members feature).**
  https://www.apa.org/members/content/outcomes-group-therapy
  _APA feature reporting the work of Burlingame (past president, APA Div. 49 and AGPA). Authority: high._
  Burlingame's **2016 meta-analysis of 46 randomized clinical trials** found **"zero difference between
  individual and group therapy across multiple disorders"** when treatments, patients, and doses are
  matched. Group therapy is **less costly**, lets a therapist "treat eight patients rather than one,"
  and "works well for all ages and across ethnic groups." Yet it is **underused** — only ~a quarter of
  psychology students in APA-approved programs get group training (often an elective). Underlying
  study: Burlingame, G. M., et al. (2016), *Psychotherapy* — individual vs. group format equivalence.

## Tier: leadership

- **[S5] Lieberman, M. A., Yalom, I. D., & Miles, M. B. (1973). *Encounter Groups: First Facts.* New York: Basic Books.**
  https://www.google.com/books/edition/Encounter_Groups_1st_Facts/KF9qAAAAMAAJ
  _The most-cited empirical study of group-leader behavior; the source of the four-functions model. Book, offline — corroborated by S1 and S6 + the primary publication record. Authority: high._
  Studied **18 encounter groups** across ten approaches. Identified four basic **leadership functions**:
  **(1) emotional stimulation** (challenging, confronting, self-disclosure, modeling risk),
  **(2) caring** (support, warmth, acceptance, protection), **(3) meaning-attribution** (explaining,
  clarifying, interpreting, providing cognitive frameworks), **(4) executive function** (setting
  limits, rules, norms, goals, managing time and structure). **Caring and meaning-attribution had a
  positive LINEAR relationship with outcome** (more is better); **emotional stimulation and executive
  function had a CURVILINEAR (inverted-U) relationship** (a moderate amount was best; too much or too
  little was worse). The **most damaging leaders were "aggressive stimulators"** — high emotional
  stimulation, intrusive, charismatic-authoritarian — and the study found real **casualties**, the
  key evidence that group leadership can harm.

- **[S6] Irish Association of Humanistic & Integrative Psychotherapy (IAHIP). *Encounter Groups Revisited.***
  https://iahip.org/page-1076325
  _Professional-body review essay revisiting Lieberman/Yalom/Miles. Authority: moderate — used to corroborate the linear/curvilinear finding._
  "The more caring… by the leader, the more positive the outcome"; "the more meaning attribution…
  the more positive the outcome"; "too much or too little of functions 1 and 4 [emotional stimulation,
  executive function]… resulted in less positive outcomes." **"The most successful leader… was one
  moderate in amount of stimulation and in expression of executive function and high in caring and
  meaning attribution."**

## Tier: stages of group development

- **[S7] Tuckman, B. W. (1965). *Developmental sequence in small groups.* Psychological Bulletin, 63(6), 384–399; Tuckman, B. W., & Jensen, M. A. C. (1977). *Stages of small-group development revisited.* Group & Organization Studies, 2(4), 419–427.**
  https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/social-sciences-and-humanities/tuckmans-stages-group-development
  _The primary model of small-group development, via the EBSCO Research Starter summary + the primary publication record. Authority: high._
  The 1965 paper named the four-stage sequence **Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing**; the 1977
  revision added a fifth stage, **Adjourning** (mourning/termination). Widely used as the generic
  developmental spine that Corey's and Yalom's clinical stage models parallel.

- **[S8] Corey, M. S., Corey, G., & Corey, C. (2018). *Groups: Process and Practice* (10th ed.); Corey, G. *Theory and Practice of Group Counseling* (9th ed.). Cengage.**
  https://www.cengage.com/c/groups-process-and-practice-10e-corey/
  _The syllabus's alternate anchor text and the standard counselor-education group text. Book, offline — corroborated by S9. Authority: high._
  The clinical stage sequence used in counselor training: **pre-group (formation/screening) →
  initial (orientation, trust, norms) → transition (anxiety, defensiveness, conflict, the struggle
  for control — the "storming" analog) → working (cohesion, productivity, risk-taking) → final
  (consolidation, termination, transfer of learning)**. The leader's tasks shift by stage.

- **[S9] Psychology Town. *The Group Counseling Process: Dynamics and Stages.***
  https://psychology.town/assessment-counselling-guidance/group-counseling-process-dynamics-stages/
  _Educational secondary — **orientation-tier**, corroborates S8's Corey stages and group-dynamics vocabulary. Authority: moderate._
  Confirms the pre-group → initial → transition → working → final sequence and the stage-specific
  member behaviors (trust vs. testing; conflict and challenges to the leader in transition; highest
  trust and risk-taking in the working stage; review and closure in the final stage).

## Tier: types of groups + the ethics of group work

- **[S10] Association for Specialists in Group Work (ASGW). *Professional Standards for the Training of Group Workers* (2000); *Best Practice Guidelines* (2007).**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK64212/
  _The ACA division that sets group-work standards; accessed via the NCBI/SAMHSA reproduction (Appendix E) + the standards documents. Authority: high (professional body)._
  Defines **four group-work specializations by focus**: **task/work groups** (accomplish an external
  goal — committees, teams), **psychoeducational groups** (guidance — teach skills/prevent, for
  relatively well-functioning members), **counseling groups** (interpersonal problem-solving and
  growth; conscious, situational, "problems of living"), and **psychotherapy groups** (remediation of
  deeper/severe dysfunction and personality reconstruction). **Best Practice Guidelines** organize the
  group worker's duties into **planning, performing, and processing**. **Screening + informed consent**
  are recommended so "the purpose and goals of the group are compatible with the needs of the members"
  — select members "who will not impede the group process, and whose well-being will not be jeopardized
  by the group experience." Note: **privilege (legal confidentiality) generally does not apply to
  group treatment unless provided by state statute** (ASGW, 2008).

- **[S11] American Counseling Association. *ACA Code of Ethics* (2014). Section B.4.a (Group Work); A.8 (Group Work: screening / protecting clients).**
  https://www.counseling.org/resources/aca-code-of-ethics.pdf
  _The profession's primary ethical document (the Unit 3 anchor). Authority: very high._
  **B.4.a, verbatim:** "In group work, counselors clearly explain the importance and parameters of
  confidentiality for the specific group being entered." The counselor **cannot guarantee** other
  members will keep confidentiality — only explain its importance, set it as a norm, and name the
  limits. A.8 charges counselors to **screen** prospective group members and to **take precautions to
  protect** members from physical, emotional, or psychological trauma.

- **[S12] Psychology Town. *Understanding Different Types of Counseling Groups.***
  https://psychology.town/counselling-interventions/understanding-types-of-counseling-groups/
  _Educational secondary — **orientation-tier**, corroborates S10's four-type taxonomy and the counseling-vs-psychotherapy distinction. Authority: moderate._
  Confirms the ASGW four types and the key hinge: **counseling groups are not aimed at major
  personality change** — they deal with conscious, situational "problems of living" — whereas
  **psychotherapy groups** target deeper, more enduring dysfunction and reconstruction.
