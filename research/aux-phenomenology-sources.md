# Sources — Elective module: Phenomenology (Husserl → Heidegger → Merleau-Ponty → the clinic)

Evidence base for [`../wiki/aux-phenomenology.md`](../wiki/aux-phenomenology.md) and its concept, theory, study, and person pages. Each entry has a one-line authority note. This is an **elective/ad-hoc module** (off the 12-unit syllabus spine); the same triangulation rules apply as for any unit.

Two kinds of source live here, and they carry different weight. **Philosophical** claims (what Husserl or Heidegger meant) are anchored to the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (SEP), the peer-reviewed reference work of record, and cross-checked against primary texts. **Clinical and empirical** claims (what phenomenology shows about depression or schizophrenia, whether self-disorders predict psychosis) are anchored to peer-reviewed journals. Where a claim rests on a single scholar's reading, the page says so.

Sibling module: existentialism is built separately as `aux-existentialism` (sources in `aux-existentialism-sources.md`). This file does not duplicate its citations; pages link across instead.

## Philosophy of phenomenology — reference works

- **[S1] Smith, D. W. (2013 rev.). "Phenomenology." _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy._** <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/phenomenology/>
  — *Peer-reviewed reference work of record. Definition of phenomenology as the study of structures of experience from the first-person point of view; intentionality as its central structure; the Brentano → Husserl → Heidegger → Merleau-Ponty → Sartre lineage and how each differs (transcendental vs. existential/hermeneutic vs. embodied); neurophenomenology as a contested recent development.*

- **[S2] "Edmund Husserl." _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_ (new entry, 2025).** <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/>
  — *SEP. Husserl's dates (1859–1938); anti-psychologism in the* Logical Investigations *(1900–01); epoché and reduction in* Ideas I *(1913); noesis/noema; eidetic variation; time-consciousness (primal impression / retention / protention); empathy and intersubjectivity (Fifth Cartesian Meditation); the lifeworld and the* Crisis *(1936).*

- **[S3] "Martin Heidegger." _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_ (new entry, 2025).** <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/>
  — *SEP. Dasein; being-in-the-world as a unitary phenomenon; the available/ready-to-hand vs. occurrent/present-at-hand; thrownness; mood (Befindlichkeit); understanding as projection; falling and das Man; care; anxiety vs. fear; being-toward-death; authenticity. Also the entry's treatment of Heidegger's Nazism, antisemitism, and the Black Notebooks, and the unresolved scholarly debate about how far it infects the philosophy.*

- **[S4] "Maurice Merleau-Ponty." _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_ (2016; substantive revision 2023).** <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/>
  — *SEP. The lived body (le corps propre); body schema vs. body image; motor intentionality ("I can," not "I think"); habit and the habit-body; the Schneider case; the phantom limb; the primacy of perception; flesh; Merleau-Ponty's use of Gestalt psychology, neurology, and child psychology (Sorbonne courses 1949–52).*

- **[S5] Shapiro, L. (2021). "Embodied Cognition." _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy._** <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/embodied-cognition/>
  — *SEP. Embodied cognition vs. classical cognitivism; the "4E" family (embodied, embedded, enactive, extended); Gibson's ecological psychology as precursor; Varela, Thompson & Rosch (1991) and enactivism; Clark & Chalmers's parity principle and Otto's notebook; Adams & Aizawa's coupling-constitution fallacy objection and Clark's reply.*

## Embodiment, ecology, and "the environment as part of you"

- **[S6] Clark, A., & Chalmers, D. (1998). The extended mind. _Analysis_, 58(1), 7–19.** <http://fitelson.org/prosem/clark_chalmers.pdf>
  — *Primary source; one of the most cited philosophy-of-mind papers of the past 30 years. Active externalism, the parity principle, and the Otto/Inga thought experiment.*

- **[S7] Gibson, J. J. (1979). _The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception._ Boston: Houghton Mifflin. Ch. 8, "The Theory of Affordances."** <https://monoskop.org/images/c/c6/Gibson_James_J_1977_1979_The_Theory_of_Affordances.pdf>
  — *Primary source. The defining quotation: "The affordances of the environment are what it offers the animal, what it provides or furnishes, either for good or ill," and the claim that affordances imply the complementarity of animal and environment.*

- **[S8] Sedgwick, H. A., & Rogers, B. (2022). An introduction to the special issue: The ecological approach of James J. Gibson: 40 years later. _i-Perception_, 13(5), 1–5.** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9549194/>
  — *Peer-reviewed perception journal. Gibson's direct perception vs. the inferential tradition (Helmholtz, Gregory, Rock); the editors note that opponents of the approach did not contribute to the special issue, which confirms the debate is still live.*

- **[S9] Colombetti, G., & Krueger, J. (2015). Scaffoldings of the affective mind. _Philosophical Psychology_, 28(8), 1157–1176.** <https://philpapers.org/rec/COLSOT-3>
  — *Peer-reviewed. Introduces "affective scaffolding": emotions and moods are supported and regulated by material culture, other people, and their interplay. The best academic statement of the idea that your environment is part of your emotional life.*

- **[S10] Burnes, B., & Cooke, B. (2013). Kurt Lewin's field theory: A review and re-evaluation. _International Journal of Management Reviews_, 15(4), 408–425.** <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2370.2012.00348.x>
  — *Peer-reviewed historical review. Origins of Lewin's field theory in Gestalt psychology, topology, and Cassirer; its decline after Lewin's death; the degradation of field theory into "force-field analysis."*

- **[S11] Lewin, K. (1936). _Principles of Topological Psychology._ New York: McGraw-Hill.**
  — *Primary source for B = f(P, E) and the "life space" (the totality of psychological facts, person plus psychological environment, that determine behavior at a moment). Cited through [S10] and standard social-psychology histories.*

- **[S51] Bronfenbrenner, U. (1979). _The Ecology of Human Development: Experiments by Nature and Design._ Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press.**
  — *Primary source. Bronfenbrenner explicitly adapts Lewin's B = f(PE) into a formula for development and builds the nested-systems ecological model on Lewin's field theory. Standard in developmental and counseling curricula.*

- **[S12] Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). _The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience._ MIT Press.**
  — *Primary founding text of enactivism, which brings phenomenology and cognitive science together. Cited via [S5].*

- **[S13] Adams, F., & Aizawa, K. (2008). _The Bounds of Cognition._ Blackwell.**
  — *The main critique of extended cognition (the coupling-constitution fallacy). Cited via [S5].*

## Daseinsanalysis and existential-phenomenological psychiatry

- **[S14] International Federation of Daseinsanalysis (IFDA). "What about Daseinsanalysis."** <https://i-f-da.org/what-about/>
  — *The professional federation of Daseinsanalysts (founded 1990). Authoritative for how the school describes itself: origins (Binswanger, Boss, Heidegger), the Zollikon Seminars, dreams as manifestations of being-in-the-world, and training requirements. As a self-description it is not neutral on efficacy.*

- **[S15] May, R., Angel, E., & Ellenberger, H. F. (Eds.) (1958). _Existence: A New Dimension in Psychiatry and Psychology._ New York: Basic Books.**
  — *The landmark anthology that brought European phenomenological psychiatry to American readers. It contains English translations of Binswanger's "The Case of Ellen West" and his essay on the existential-analysis school, including the Umwelt/Mitwelt/Eigenwelt triad as May presents it. Primary source.*

- **[S16] Heidegger, M. (1987/2001). _Zollikon Seminars: Protocols–Conversations–Letters_ (M. Boss, Ed.; F. Mayr & R. Askay, Trans.). Northwestern University Press.**
  — *Primary source: Heidegger's 1959–1969 seminars for psychiatrists at Boss's home in Zollikon. Includes Heidegger's criticism of Binswanger's reading of* Being and Time*. Cited via [S14] and [S17].*

- **[S17] Olesen, J. (2006). Boss' and Binswanger's health anthropologies and existential philosophies. _Philosophical Practice_, 2(2), 99–109.** <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17428170601095457> Also: Ghaemi, S. N. (2001). Rediscovering existential psychotherapy: The contribution of Ludwig Binswanger. _American Journal of Psychotherapy_, 55(1), 51–64. <https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/pdf/10.1176/appi.psychotherapy.2001.55.1.51>
  — *Peer-reviewed secondary literature. Heidegger rejected Binswanger's appropriation as conflating the ontological level with the everyday (ontic) level of existence. After a 1957 Madrid conference Boss published a critique of Freud, Jung, and Binswanger, and the two men split; Binswanger then leaned more on Husserl, while Heidegger backed Boss. Ghaemi (a psychiatrist) makes the case for Binswanger's continuing clinical relevance. Used for the rift and the reception, cross-checked against [S14].*

- **[S18] Boss, M. (1958). _The Analysis of Dreams._ London: Rider.** Also: Gendlin, E. T. "Phenomenological concept versus phenomenological method: A critique of Medard Boss on dreams." <https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2045.html>
  — *Primary source for Boss's anti-reductive dream method: take the manifest dream as it shows itself and do not decode it. Gendlin's critique (Gendlin was Rogers's collaborator and the originator of Focusing) is the standard objection that Boss replaced one set of imposed concepts with another.*

## The Ellen West case

- **[S19] Akavia, N. (2008). Writing "The Case of Ellen West": Clinical knowledge and historical representation. _Science in Context_, 21(1), 119–144.** <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5308050_Writing_The_Case_of_Ellen_West_Clinical_Knowledge_and_Historical_Representation>
  — *Peer-reviewed history of science. Uses archival material to show how Binswanger's published case constructs its subject, and that the archive gives a different picture of Ellen West than the published text.*

- **[S20] Hirschmüller, A. (Ed.) (2003). _Ellen West: Eine Patientin Ludwig Binswangers zwischen Kreativität und destruktivem Leiden._ Heidelberg: Asanger.** Reported in English in: "New documentation on the famous case of 'Ellen West'." _Eating Disorders Review_ (IAEDP Foundation). <https://edr.iaedpfoundation.com/new-documentation-on-the-famous-case-of-ellen-west/>
  — *A medical historian's edition of the original hospital files, Binswanger's notes (Tübingen archive), and family correspondence. Findings as reported: the presentation fits anorexia nervosa, binge-purge type, better than Binswanger's schizophrenia diagnosis; some evidence suggests the discharge was effectively arranged around a foreseeable suicide. The English report is a professional-society summary, not the primary text, so these conclusions are marked as reported.*

- **[S21] Rogers, C. R. (1961/1980). "Ellen West — and loneliness." _Review of Existential Psychology and Psychiatry_; reprinted in _A Way of Being_ (1980). Houghton Mifflin.**
  — *Primary source for the person-centered critique: Ellen West was treated as an object of diagnosis and never met in a relationship. Cited as widely summarized; see also the orientation-only Wikipedia entry "Ellen West."*

## Phenomenological psychopathology

- **[S22] Kumazaki, T. (2013). The theoretical root of Karl Jaspers' _General Psychopathology_. Part 1: Reconsidering the influence of phenomenology and hermeneutics. _History of Psychiatry_, 24(2), 212–226.** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4995496/>
  — *Peer-reviewed history of psychiatry. Jaspers's phenomenology as static understanding (selecting, delimiting, and describing experiences from the patient's own accounts); genetic understanding; the switch to causal explanation where meaningful connection breaks down; and the scholarly debate over how Husserlian Jaspers really was (his empirical descriptive psychology differs from Husserl's eidetic method).*

- **[S23] Thome, J. (2014). Centenary of Karl Jaspers's _General Psychopathology_: implications for molecular psychiatry. _Journal of Molecular Psychiatry_, 2(1), 3.** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4223880/>
  — *Peer-reviewed. Verstehen (subjective inner world) vs. Erklären (objective, causal) as still foundational to psychopathology. Argues neurobiological psychiatry needs Jaspers's methodological self-reflection.*

- **[S24] Sass, L. A., & Byrom, G. (2015). Phenomenological and neurocognitive perspectives on delusions: A critical overview. _World Psychiatry_, 14(2), 164–173.** <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wps.20205>
  — *Peer-reviewed; World Psychiatry is the WPA's flagship journal. Jaspers's primary delusion as un-understandable (not derivable from prior mental content) and delusional perception, weighed against neurocognitive accounts.*

- **[S25] Mishara, A. L., & Fusar-Poli, P. (2013). The phenomenology and neurobiology of delusion formation during psychosis onset: Jaspers, Truman symptoms, and aberrant salience. _Schizophrenia Bulletin_, 39(2), 278–286.** <https://academic.oup.com/schizophreniabulletin/article-abstract/39/2/278/1827432>
  — *Peer-reviewed; top schizophrenia journal. Links Jaspers's delusional mood and primary delusion to aberrant-salience neurobiology. A worked example of phenomenology and neuroscience in dialogue.*

- **[S26] Abe, Y., & Raballo, A. (2013). Minkowski revisited: Glancing at the clinical core of schizophrenic vulnerability. _Innovations in Clinical Neuroscience_, 10(3), 10–11.** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3638845/>
  — *Peer-reviewed clinical note. Minkowski's "loss of vital contact with reality," morbid rationalism, and the generative disorder (trouble générateur) beneath manifest symptoms.*

- **[S27] Urfer, A. (2001). Phenomenology and psychopathology of schizophrenia: The views of Eugène Minkowski. _Philosophy, Psychiatry, & Psychology_, 8(4), 279–289.** <https://muse.jhu.edu/article/37105>
  — *Peer-reviewed; PPP is the main philosophy-of-psychiatry journal. Minkowski's lived time and space, the Bergson/Husserl/Scheler influences, and loss of vital contact as a change in the temporo-spatial structure of experience.*

- **[S28] Guardascione, A. (2023). Selfhood and alterity: Schizophrenic experience between Blankenburg and Tatossian. _Frontiers in Psychology_, 14, 1214474.** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10361292/>
  — *Peer-reviewed. Blankenburg's "loss of natural self-evidence" (the Anne Rau case) as a disorder of common sense and intersubjectivity, contrasted with Tatossian's intrasubjective reading.*

- **[S29] Sass, L. A., & Parnas, J. (2003). Schizophrenia, consciousness, and the self. _Schizophrenia Bulletin_, 29(3), 427–444.** <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14609238/>
  — *Peer-reviewed landmark paper. The ipseity-disturbance model: schizophrenia as a disorder of basic selfhood, with hyperreflexivity and diminished self-affection.*

- **[S30] Parnas, J., Møller, P., Kircher, T., Thalbitzer, J., Jansson, L., Handest, P., & Zahavi, D. (2005). EASE: Examination of Anomalous Self-Experience. _Psychopathology_, 38(5), 236–258.**
  — *Peer-reviewed. The semi-structured interview that made self-disorders measurable. Cited via [S31] and [S33].*

- **[S31] Raballo, A., Henriksen, M. G., Poletti, M., & Parnas, J. (2025). "Schizophrenia, consciousness, and the self" twenty years later: Revisiting the ipseity-disturbance model and the developmental nature of self-disorder in the schizophrenia spectrum. _Schizophrenia Bulletin_, 51(5), 1187–1192.** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12414551/>
  — *Peer-reviewed 20-year update by the model's own school. Adds a third component (disturbed grip or hold on the world). Meta-analytic evidence that self-disorders cluster in the schizophrenia spectrum and not in other disorders, and predict transition to psychosis in help-seeking youth. Acknowledges open challenges (Feyaerts & Sass): specificity, independence from interpersonal context, neural correlates. **Authored by proponents; weight accordingly.***

- **[S32] Fuchs, T. (2005). Corporealized and disembodied minds: A phenomenological view of the body in melancholia and schizophrenia. _Philosophy, Psychiatry, & Psychology_, 12(2), 95–107.** <https://philpapers.org/rec/FUCCAD>
  — *Peer-reviewed; Fuchs is the leading figure in contemporary phenomenological psychiatry (Heidelberg, Karl Jaspers chair). The lived body as a transparent medium; depression as corporealization (the body turns opaque, heavy, an obstacle); schizophrenia as disembodiment.*

- **[S33] Frohn, O. O., & Martiny, K. M. (2023). The phenomenological model of depression: From methodological challenges to clinical advancements. _Frontiers in Psychology_, 14, 1215388.** <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1215388/full>
  — *Peer-reviewed synthesis. Fuchs (corporealization, desynchronization), Ratcliffe (existential feeling), the lived-time tradition (Minkowski, Straus, von Gebsattel), Tellenbach's typus melancholicus, a flattened field of affordances. States the key limitation: the model leans on autobiographical and literary descriptions, and depression distorts the very self-report it depends on.*

- **[S34] Ratcliffe, M. (2015). _Experiences of Depression: A Study in Phenomenology._ Oxford University Press.** Review: Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews. <https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/experiences-of-depression-a-study-in-phenomenology/>
  — *Academic monograph built partly on a survey of first-person accounts of depression. Existential feelings; depression as a change in the space of possibilities (loss of hope as the loss of the capacity to hope, not just the absence of hopes). NDPR is the standard peer-review venue for philosophy books.*

- **[S50] Fernandez, A. V. (2014). Depression as existential feeling or de-situatedness? Distinguishing structure from mode in psychopathology. _Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences_, 13(4), 595–612.** <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-014-9374-y>
  — *Peer-reviewed critique of Ratcliffe. Argues depression is better described as a change in the* mode *of being-situated than as an existential feeling; shows the concept is actively debated.*

## Empathy and intersubjectivity

- **[S35] Stein, E. (1917/1989). _On the Problem of Empathy_ (W. Stein, Trans.). ICS Publications.** Secondary: Svenaeus, F. (2018). Edith Stein's phenomenology of sensual and emotional empathy. _Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences_, 17, 741–760. <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-017-9544-9>
  — *Primary text (Stein's dissertation under Husserl) plus a peer-reviewed reading. Empathy as a sui generis experience of another's experience that is given directly yet as the other's, not mine. Distinguished from emotional contagion, sympathy, and inference.*

- **[S36] Zahavi, D. (2014). _Self and Other: Exploring Subjectivity, Empathy, and Shame._ Oxford University Press.**
  — *Academic monograph by the leading contemporary phenomenologist (Copenhagen, Center for Subjectivity Research). Empathy as direct, perception-like acquaintance with others' minds, against simulation and theory-theory. Zahavi's example: seeing someone's anger may make me afraid, not angry, yet I still grasp their anger. Cross-checked against [S2] and [S35].*

## The phenomenological stance in counseling and therapy

- **[S37] Rogers, C. R. (1951). _Client-Centered Therapy._ Boston: Houghton Mifflin (the 19 propositions).**
  — *Primary source. Rogers called his theory of personality "basically phenomenological in character": the phenomenal field and the internal frame of reference as the vantage point for understanding behavior. Corroborated by [`unit01 sources`](unit01-helping-skills-sources.md) and the existing [[person-carl-rogers]] page.*

- **[S38] Yontef, G. (1993). _Awareness, Dialogue & Process: Essays on Gestalt Therapy._ Gestalt Journal Press. Introductory chapter online: <http://gestalt.org/yontef.htm>**
  — *Leading theorist of relational Gestalt therapy. The phenomenological method of awareness (perceiving and feeling distinguished from interpreting; what and how over why); Buber's dialogic relationship (inclusion, presence); field theory.*

- **[S39] Spinelli, E. (2005). _The Interpreted World: An Introduction to Phenomenological Psychology_ (2nd ed.). London: Sage.**
  — *Standard text of the British existential-phenomenological school. Source for the three-step phenomenological method used in therapy: the rule of epoché, the rule of description, and the rule of horizontalization. Corroborated by the Wiley World Handbook of Existential Therapy chapter on existential-phenomenological therapy.*

## Phenomenological research methods

- **[S40] Giorgi, A. (2009). _The Descriptive Phenomenological Method in Psychology: A Modified Husserlian Approach._ Pittsburgh: Duquesne University Press.**
  — *Primary methods text by the founder of phenomenological psychology as a human science (Duquesne school). Steps: adopt the phenomenological attitude; read for the whole; delineate meaning units; transform them into psychologically sensitive expressions; synthesize a general structure.*

- **[S41] Smith, J. A., Flowers, P., & Larkin, M. (2009; 2nd ed. 2022). _Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis: Theory, Method and Research._ London: Sage.**
  — *The defining IPA text. Three theoretical touchstones (phenomenology, hermeneutics, idiography); the double hermeneutic; small, homogeneous, purposive samples.*

- **[S42] van Manen, M. (1990). _Researching Lived Experience: Human Science for an Action Sensitive Pedagogy._ Albany: SUNY Press.**
  — *Primary text of hermeneutic phenomenology as a research approach. Follows Heidegger and Gadamer: pre-understanding cannot be fully bracketed and must be made explicit.*

- **[S43] Moustakas, C. (1994). _Phenomenological Research Methods._ Thousand Oaks, CA: Sage.**
  — *Primary text of "transcendental" (Husserl-inspired) research phenomenology: epoché, horizontalization of significant statements, clusters of meaning, textural (what) and structural (how) descriptions, and a composite essence. The version most counseling dissertations follow (via Creswell).*

- **[S44] Creswell, J. W., & Poth, C. N. (2018). _Qualitative Inquiry and Research Design: Choosing Among Five Approaches_ (4th ed.). Sage.**
  — *The standard counseling and education qualitative-methods textbook on CACREP research syllabi. Presents phenomenology as one of five approaches (with narrative, grounded theory, ethnography, and case study), largely in Moustakas's version.*

- **[S45] Oluka, A. (2025). Phenomenological research strategy: Descriptive and interpretive approaches. _F1000Research_, 14, 725.** <https://f1000research.com/articles/14-725>
  — *Open peer-reviewed overview. Its status is "2 approved with reservations", so it is used for orientation and cross-checking only. Descriptive vs. interpretive traditions, imaginative variation, Gadamer's fusion of horizons, sample-size conventions (Polkinghorne 5–25; IPA roughly 3–10, single cases valued), and LeVasseur's "bridling" as an alternative to strict bracketing.*

- **[S46] Zahavi, D. (2019). Getting it quite wrong: Van Manen and Smith on phenomenology. _Qualitative Health Research_, 29(6), 900–907.** <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30565516/>
  — *Peer-reviewed critique by a leading philosophical phenomenologist. Argues that both van Manen's and Smith's (IPA's) accounts of phenomenology spread confusions about what phenomenology is, including the role of the epoché and reduction in empirical research. The center of the ongoing "is applied phenomenology really phenomenology?" debate.*

## Primary philosophical texts (cited by page for specific formulations)

- **[S48] Merleau-Ponty, M. (1945/2012). _Phenomenology of Perception_ (D. A. Landes, Trans.). Routledge.**
  — *Primary text. The lived body, body schema, motor intentionality, the Schneider and phantom-limb analyses, and the Preface's remark that "the most important lesson of the reduction is the impossibility of a complete reduction." Cross-checked against [S4].*

  — *Primary text for Dasein and being-in-the-world. The pages rely on [S3] for interpretation; the primary text is cited for terminology only.*
