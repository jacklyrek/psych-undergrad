# Sources — Elective module: Psychological First Aid & Acute Grief Support

Evidence base for [`../wiki/aux-psychological-first-aid.md`](../wiki/aux-psychological-first-aid.md)
and its concept pages. Each entry has a one-line authority note. This is an **elective/ad-hoc
module** (off the 12-unit syllabus spine); it leans on the same triangulation rules as any unit.
Clinically heavy topic — claims are kept precise and tied to professional bodies and peer-reviewed
work, not SEO grief blogs.

---

## Psychological First Aid frameworks

- **[S1] WHO, War Trauma Foundation & World Vision International (2011). _Psychological First Aid:
  Guide for Field Workers._ Geneva: World Health Organization.**
  WHO IRIS: <https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/e7e129fb-b306-496d-84a5-67bb70abc130/content>
  — *The global consensus PFA guide; endorsed by 24+ international humanitarian agencies. Source for
  the **Look / Listen / Link** action principles and the "humane, supportive, practical help" framing.*

- **[S2] National Child Traumatic Stress Network (NCTSN) & National Center for PTSD (2006).
  _Psychological First Aid: Field Operations Guide_, 2nd ed.**
  <https://www.nctsn.org/resources/psychological-first-aid-pfa-field-operations-guide-2nd-edition>
  — *The U.S. evidence-informed standard, co-developed by the two leading American trauma bodies.
  Source for the **eight Core Actions**.*

- **[S3] U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, National Center for PTSD — _Psychological First Aid_
  (provider page).**
  <https://www.ptsd.va.gov/disaster_events/for_providers/psychological_first_aid.asp>
  — *`.gov` clinical reference. Confirms the 8 Core Actions and the objective "to reduce initial
  distress and foster short- and long-term adaptive functioning," and that PFA **does not require
  survivors to talk about their experience**.*

## Why PFA is not debriefing (the evidence that reshaped acute response)

- **[S4] Rose S, Bisson J, Churchill R, Wessely S. _Psychological debriefing for preventing post
  traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)._ Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.**
  <https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000560_psychological-debriefing-preventing-post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd>
  — *Gold-standard systematic review. Single-session individual psychological debriefing is **not**
  useful for preventing PTSD and **may increase risk** of PTSD/depression in some.*

- **[S5] National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) — PTSD guideline (NG116).**
  — *UK national clinical guideline: **do not offer** psychologically-focused debriefing for the
  prevention or treatment of PTSD. (Cited via [S4] and NICE summaries; corroborates the Cochrane
  conclusion at guideline level.)*

## Grief and bereavement models

- **[S6] "Understanding grief and bereavement." PMC / NIH (PMC5033290).**
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5033290/>
  — *Peer-reviewed overview surveying Kübler-Ross, Freud's grief work, the dual process model,
  resilience/meaning-making, and complicated-grief features.*

- **[S7] Stroebe M & Schut H (1999). The Dual Process Model of coping with bereavement.**
  — *Foundational peer-reviewed model: oscillation between **loss-oriented** and
  **restoration-oriented** coping. (Described in detail in [S6] and [S9].)*

- **[S8] Worden JW. _Grief Counseling and Grief Therapy._**
  — *Standard clinical text; source for the **Four Tasks of Mourning** (accept the reality of the
  loss; process the pain; adjust to a world without the deceased; find an enduring connection while
  moving forward).*

- **[S9] Stroebe M et al. / "Stages of Grief Portrayed on the Internet: A Systematic Analysis and
  Critical Appraisal." Frontiers in Psychology (PMC8675126).**
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8675126/>
  — *Peer-reviewed critical appraisal of the Kübler-Ross stage model: built from interviews with
  **dying patients, not the bereaved**; grief does not follow a fixed order; popular misuse can make
  people feel they are "grieving wrong."*

## Prolonged grief disorder (the clinical boundary)

- **[S10] Prigerson HG et al. (2021). Validation of the new DSM-5-TR criteria for prolonged grief
  disorder and the PG-13-Revised scale. _World Psychiatry_.**
  <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wps.20823>
  — *Peer-reviewed validation of the PGD criteria.*

- **[S11] "Prolonged grief disorder in DSM-5-TR: early predictors." PMC9131400.**
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9131400/>
  — *Peer-reviewed: **loss of a child** and loss to **unnatural/violent** causes are among the
  strongest predictors of PGD; unpreparedness for the death raises risk.*

- **[S12] DSM-5-TR (American Psychiatric Association, 2022) — Prolonged Grief Disorder.**
  — *Diagnostic authority: the **12-month** (adult) / **6-month** (child/adolescent) threshold and
  the core symptoms (intense yearning/preoccupation). Cited via [S10][S11] and APA materials.*

## Practical support of the bereaved (incl. death of a child)

- **[S13] Susan Silk & Barry Goldman (2013), "How not to say the wrong thing," _Los Angeles Times_;
  explainer via Psych Central.**
  <https://psychcentral.com/health/circle-of-grief-ring-theory>
  — *Origin of **Ring Theory** ("comfort IN, dump OUT"): support flows toward the center of the
  crisis, complaints flow outward. Widely adopted clinical heuristic.*

- **[S14] Nationwide Children's Hospital — "What to Say and What Not to Say to a Grieving Parent"
  (700 Children's, clinically reviewed).**
  <https://www.nationwidechildrens.org/family-resources-education/700childrens/2023/07/grieving-a-child>
  — *Pediatric-hospital patient-education: acknowledge the child, **say the child's name**, follow
  the parent's lead, check in over the long haul; avoid "they're in a better place," "everything
  happens for a reason," "I know how you feel."*

- **[S15] Children's Hospital Colorado — "How to Help a Grieving Parent."**
  <https://www.childrenscolorado.org/doctors-and-departments/departments/grief-and-loss/how-to-help-a-grieving-parent/>
  — *Corroborating hospital grief-program guidance: presence over fixing, tolerate silence, mark
  anniversaries/birthdays, offer concrete help rather than "let me know if you need anything."*

## Death notification (added 2026-07-08)

- **[S16] Hobgood C, Harward D, Newton K, Davis W (2005). "The educational intervention 'GRIEV_ING'
  improves the death notification skills of residents." _Academic Emergency Medicine_ 12(4):296–301.**
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15805319/>
  — *Peer-reviewed origin of the **GRIEV_ING** death-notification protocol; a defined training built
  on it measurably improved resident physicians' confidence and competence.*

- **[S17] American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) — _Delivering the News with Compassion:
  The GRIEV_ING Death Notification Protocol_ (trainer's manual).**
  <https://www.acep.org/siteassets/uploads/uploaded-files/acep/clinical-and-practice-management/resources/academics/deathnotificationtrainersmanual.pdf>
  — *Professional-body training standard; full expansion of the mnemonic (Gather, Resources,
  Identify, Educate, **Verify with the words "died"/"dead"**, _space_, Inquire, Nuts-and-bolts,
  Give), incl. the explicit instruction to avoid euphemisms.*

- **[S18] MADD (Mothers Against Drunk Driving), adopted by the U.S. DOJ Office for Victims of Crime —
  _Death Notification: Breaking the Bad News with Concern for the Professional and Compassion for
  the Survivor_ (training curriculum).**
  Described in: <https://www.officer.com/home/article/10249064/death-notification-breaking-the-bad-news>
  — *The de-facto law-enforcement standard, source of the five principles: **in person, in time, in
  pairs, in plain language, with compassion** — including "don't be afraid of the D-words; 'expired,'
  'passed on,' 'lost' are words of denial."*

- **[S19] Chapple A, Ziebland S (2010). "Viewing the body after bereavement due to a traumatic
  death: qualitative study in the UK." _BMJ_ 340:c2032.**
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2862150/>
  — *Peer-reviewed qualitative study (n=80 bereaved by suicide/traumatic death): of 49 who viewed,
  35 said it was right, 2 regretted it (both with badly damaged bodies and no real choice); some
  non-viewers later wished they had. Conclusion: offer a **prepared, genuine choice** — "clinicians
  should not assume that relatives will be harmed by seeing a bruised or damaged body."*

- **[S20] McCarroll JE et al. (2024). "Notification, viewing the body, and social and cultural
  considerations after traumatic death: a systematic literature review." _Disaster Medicine and
  Public Health Preparedness_.**
  <https://doi.org/10.1017/dmp.2024.209> (Cambridge Core; paywalled)
  — *Recent systematic review confirming notification + viewing-the-body as an evidence topic;
  **abstract-level orientation only** (full text not consulted).*

- **[S2 — additional use]** The NCTSN/NCPTSD *PFA Field Operations Guide* (see above) contains the
  acute-response sections this page leans on: *when a family member is missing* (accurate
  information, no premature reassurance), *when a death occurs*, *support for death notification*,
  and *support for body identification* (Core Action 2, Safety and Comfort).
