# Unit 2 sources — Counseling Theories: The Major Schools

Evidence base for Unit 2 readings. Each source has a one-line authority note. Inline citations in
wiki pages reference these numbers, e.g. `[S5]`.

_Researched 2026-06-27. Triangulated across NCBI/StatPearls peer-reviewed clinical references, an
open textbook (OpenStax), peer-reviewed journal articles (PMC), an academic book chapter, and
encyclopedic references. Lower-tier training/blog sites used for orientation/wording only and

## Tier: peer-reviewed / professional reference (lean on these)

- **[S1] StatPearls — *Psychodynamic Therapy*, NCBI Bookshelf (NBK606117).**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK606117/
  _Peer-reviewed clinical reference (NCBI). Authority: high._
  Key points: psychodynamic theory holds that **"unconscious thoughts, desires, and memories
  significantly impact behavior"**; the unconscious is **"a vast reservoir of often repressed or
  forgotten material that continues to influence behaviors and emotions, even if people are unaware
  of its influence."** Emphasizes **unresolved conflicts** from early childhood. **Defense
  mechanisms** = "unconscious psychological strategies that individuals use to cope with anxiety";
  Anna Freud distinguished adaptive vs. maladaptive. **Transference** = "unconscious redirection of
  feelings, expectations, and relational patterns from past relationships—especially early
  ones—onto the therapist"; **countertransference** = the clinician's unconscious reactions to the
  patient. Change mechanisms: **insight, affect (emotional processing), and the therapeutic
  alliance.** Lineage: Freud (1890s) → Anna Freud / ego psychology → object relations.
  *Limitations:* effective for depression/anxiety but "limited or less robust" evidence for OCD,
  PTSD, psychosis; criticized for difficulty of empirical validation and cost/length.

- **[S2] StatPearls — *Defense Mechanisms*, NCBI Bookshelf (NBK559106).**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559106/
  _Peer-reviewed clinical reference (NCBI). Authority: high._
  Defense mechanisms as unconscious anxiety-management; named exemplars: repression, denial,
  projection, displacement, sublimation, rationalization, reaction formation. Used for the
  defense-mechanism examples in the psychodynamic page.

- **[S3] StatPearls — *Person-Centered Therapy (Rogerian Therapy)*, NCBI Bookshelf (NBK589708).**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK589708/
  _Peer-reviewed clinical reference (NCBI). Authority: high. (Shared with Unit 1.)_
  Pioneered by Rogers (early 1940s); grounded in the idea people are **"inherently motivated toward
  achieving positive psychological functioning"** (the actualizing tendency). **Client is the expert
  in their own life and leads the direction; therapist is non-directive.** Three core conditions:
  empathy, congruence, unconditional positive regard. Client problem = **incongruence** between
  self-image and experienced reality. *Limitations:* principles can be "too vague," efficacy
  evidence mixed (not inferior to CBT for depression at 6 months, possibly inferior at 12).

- **[S4] StatPearls — *Cognitive Behavior Therapy*, NCBI Bookshelf (NBK470241).**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470241/
  _Peer-reviewed clinical reference (NCBI). Authority: high._
  CBT links thoughts, emotions, behaviors; **"dysfunctional automatic thoughts that are exaggerated,
  distorted, mistaken, or unrealistic … play a significant role in psychopathology."** Three levels:
  **automatic thoughts** (immediate interpretations), **cognitive distortions** (dichotomous
  thinking, overgeneralization, catastrophizing, mind reading, personalization), **underlying
  beliefs** (intermediate conditional rules; core beliefs like "I am inadequate"). Beck developed CT
  in the 1960s after observing depressed patients' cognitive distortions, reframing depression as
  fundamentally a cognitive disorder. Typical course ≈ 8–12 weekly sessions; often combined with
  medication. Extensively researched; effective for depression, anxiety, eating disorders, substance
  abuse, personality disorders, and as an adjunct for bipolar/schizophrenia.

- **[S9] *A Historical and Theoretical Review of Cognitive Behavioral Therapies*, PMC6208646.**
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6208646/
  _Peer-reviewed (open access). Authority: high._
  REBT (Ellis, 1950s) and Cognitive Therapy (Beck, 1960s) are the two earliest CBTs. Ellis's **ABC
  model**: emotional disturbance is caused not by events (A) but by **beliefs/evaluations (B)** about
  them, producing consequences (C); REBT is "the original form and one of the main pillars" of CBT.
  Ellis emphasized the rational/irrational distinction and a more confrontational disputation style;
  Beck more collaborative, treating automatic thoughts as **hypotheses to test** (collaborative
  empiricism) rather than errors to argue away. Used for the Ellis-vs-Beck distinction.

- **[S6] Yalom, *Existential Psychotherapy* (1980) — the four ultimate concerns.**
  Corroborated via: Wikipedia summary https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_Psychotherapy_(book)
  and Yalom's own site https://www.yalom.com/existential-psychotherapy
  _Primary framework (Yalom 1980). Authority: high as a primary source; accessed here via reputable
  secondary summaries — verify exact wording against the book before quoting verbatim._
  Yalom argues psychological distress stems from confrontation with four **ultimate concerns / givens
  of existence**: (1) **death** — the inevitable, ultimate limitation; (2) **freedom** — we are the
  sole authors of our lives and therefore responsible, which is groundless and anxiety-provoking;
  (3) **isolation** — our ultimate aloneness (born and die alone); (4) **meaninglessness** — no
  given meaning, so we must create our own. Each confrontation generates an "existential dynamic
  conflict."

- **[S8] *Existential isolation* review, PMC5553124.**
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553124/
  _Peer-reviewed (open access). Authority: high (for the isolation typology)._
  Distinguishes **existential isolation** (the unbridgeable gap between self and others) from
  **interpersonal isolation** (being apart from other people) and **intrapersonal isolation**
  (disconnection from parts of oneself). Used to keep the three senses of "isolation" straight.

- **[S11] *Differentiation of self: A scoping review of Bowen Family Systems Theory's core
  construct*, ScienceDirect (2021).**
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272735821001446
  _Peer-reviewed scoping review. Authority: high (for differentiation)._
  **Differentiation of self** — the cornerstone of Bowen's model — is the capacity to maintain a
  clear sense of self and make values-based decisions while managing emotional reactivity yet
  remaining emotionally connected to others. Links to the **multigenerational transmission process**
  (people partner at similar differentiation levels; levels can decline across generations).

- **[S12] Brown (2024), *Bowen family systems theory and practice: Illustration and critique
  revisited*, Australian & New Zealand Journal of Family Therapy.**
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/anzf.1589
  _Peer-reviewed. Authority: high (for the critique)._
  Revisits Bowen theory's strengths and limitations (e.g., debates over empirical operationalization
  of differentiation, cultural/gender critiques of the differentiation ideal). Used to flag what is
  contested in family-systems theory.

## Tier: open textbook / institutional (good for structure & corroboration)

- **[S5] OpenStax, *Psychology 2e*, §16.2 *Types of Treatment*.**
  https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/16-2-types-of-treatment
  _Open textbook (peer-reviewed, Rice University). Authority: high._
  Crisp orientation contrasts used in the hub page:
  • **Psychoanalysis/psychodynamic** — "most of our psychological problems are the result of
  repressed impulses and trauma experienced in childhood"; past-oriented; directive; uses free
  association, dream analysis, transference. Modern version "less intensive than Freud's original."
  • **CBT** — "how you think determines how you feel and act"; targets cognitive distortions +
  self-defeating behaviors; **present-oriented**; directive; ABC model. Behavioral subtype: maladaptive
  responses learned via classical/operant conditioning, changed via counterconditioning, exposure,
  systematic desensitization, token economies.
  • **Humanistic/Rogerian** — client-centered, **non-directive** ("does not give advice or provide
  interpretations"); focuses on **conscious** thoughts and the present/future; uses active listening,
  UPR, genuineness, empathy; problem = incongruence between real and ideal self.
  (Note: this OpenStax section does **not** cover family-systems or postmodern therapies — those are
  sourced from S10–S14.)

- **[S7] LibreTexts (Kelland), *Personality Theory in a Cultural Context* — *Viktor Frankl and
  Logotherapy*.**
  https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Psychology/Culture_and_Community/Personality_Theory_in_a_Cultural_Context_(Kelland)/09%3A_Viktor_Frankl_Rollo_May_and_Existential_Psychology/9.03%3A_Viktor_Frankl_and_Logotherapy
  _Open textbook. Authority: medium-high._
  Frankl's **will-to-meaning** (vs. Freud's will-to-pleasure, Adler's will-to-power). Three sources
  of meaning: (1) creating/achieving through work or a deed; (2) experiencing something or
  encountering someone (esp. love); (3) **the attitude one takes toward unavoidable suffering** —
  "the last of the human freedoms." Failure to find meaning → **existential vacuum** and **noogenic
  neurosis**. Techniques: **paradoxical intention** (deliberately intensify the feared symptom to
  gain distance) and **dereflection** (redirect attention away from anxious self-monitoring).

- **[S10] EBSCO Research Starters — *Family systems theory*.**
  https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/family-systems-theory
  _Curated encyclopedic reference. Authority: medium-high._
  The family is an **emotional unit / system**; the individual's symptom is understood in the context
  of relationships, not in isolation. **Bowen** contributed differentiation and **triangulation** (a
  two-person tension recruits a third to stabilize). **Minuchin's structural family therapy** focuses
  on **family structure, subsystems, and boundaries** as enacted in the session. Source of the
  "problem lives in the relationship system" framing and the Bowen-vs-Minuchin contrast.

- **[S13] Tarragona (2008), *Postmodern/Poststructuralist Therapies* (academic book chapter).**
  https://is.muni.cz/el/fss/jaro2012/PST450/um/Tarragona_2008_Postmodern_Therapies.pdf
  _Academic book chapter (course-hosted PDF). Authority: medium-high._
  **Social constructionism:** no single objective truth; reality is **constructed through language
  and relationship**; meaning emerges in dialogue. Therapist adopts a **non-expert, collaborative**
  stance. **SFBT (de Shazer & Berg):** future-oriented, resource-focused — **miracle question,
  exception questions, scaling**. **Narrative (White & Epston):** "**the person is not the
  problem**"; **externalizing** the problem; **dominant (problem-saturated) story vs. alternative
  story**; **unique outcomes**; **re-authoring** a preferred narrative.

- **[S14] SAGE Encyclopedia of Theory in Counseling and Psychotherapy — *Solution-Focused Brief
  Therapy*.**
  https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/the-sage-encyclopedia-of-theory-in-counseling-and-psychotherapy/chpt/solutionfocused-brief-therapy
  _Reference encyclopedia (peer-reviewed editorial). Authority: high._
  SFBT as a **postmodern, constructivist, future-oriented, anti-deterministic** approach; clients are
  assumed able to change quickly and to construct "problem-free" language toward a new reality;
  cofounded by **Steve de Shazer and Insoo Kim Berg**; arose alongside early family therapy.

## Tier: orientation only (wording/teaching gloss — NOT load-bearing for claims)

- **[S15] Survey data on theoretical orientation (integrative/eclectic prevalence).**
  Aggregated via ScienceDirect topic page and a teaching summary of APA Division 12 (2010) data.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/psychology/theoretical-orientation
  _Authority: medium; treat figures as approximate._
  Across large therapist surveys, **integrative/eclectic** is consistently among the most common
  self-identified orientations; estimates vary widely by sample (one survey: only ~15% used a single
  orientation; APA Div. 12 2010: cognitive ≈ 31%, eclectic ≈ 22%; range commonly cited ~13–42%).
  Used only for the "most counselors end up integrative" claim, stated with a hedge.

## Open threads / where sources disagree or hedge
- **Psychodynamic evidence base:** supportive for depression/anxiety but weaker/contested for OCD,
  PTSD, psychosis; long-standing critique that core constructs (the unconscious, transference) are
  hard to operationalize and falsify [S1]. Flag in the reading.
- **Person-centered "sufficiency":** Rogers held the core conditions are necessary *and sufficient*;
  mainstream view is necessary-but-not-sufficient (see Unit 1 `concept-core-conditions`) [S3].
- **Differentiation of self:** central to Bowen but its **measurement and cross-cultural validity
  are debated**; the "differentiation" ideal has drawn gender/cultural critique [S11][S12].
- **Integrative/eclectic prevalence [S15]:** directionally well-supported (it's common), but exact
  percentages vary widely by survey, profession, and year — cite as "most" / "among the most common,"
  not a hard number.
- **Yalom's four givens [S6]:** a clinically influential *framework*, not an experimentally tested
  taxonomy — present it as an organizing lens, not a measured fact.
