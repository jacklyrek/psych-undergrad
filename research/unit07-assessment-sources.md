# Unit 7 sources — Assessment, Diagnosis & Case Conceptualization

Evidence base for Unit 7 readings. Each source has a one-line authority note. Inline citations in
wiki pages reference these numbers, e.g. `[S1]`.

_Researched 2026-07-17. Anchored on **peer-reviewed clinical references** (NCBI Bookshelf StatPearls
for the mental status examination and for sensitivity/specificity; the NCBI Bookshelf IOM report on
psychological testing for reliability/validity/standardization), the **instrument-validation
primaries** (Kroenke et al. 2001 for the PHQ-9; Spitzer et al. 2006 for the GAD-7, corroborated by a
peer-reviewed psychometric replication), the **anchor text's author** (Sommers-Flanagan on the
clinical interview), a **peer-reviewed counseling journal** (The Professional Counselor) for the
Five Ps case-formulation framework, and a **peer-reviewed Psychiatric Services statement** on
measurement-based care. Screening-instrument details were cross-checked against the APA's public
assessment page. Some measurement-based-care and treatment-planning specifics come from sources whose
full text 403'd to automated access (Psychiatric Services PDF, the OER reliability chapter) and are
cited via their abstracts / search-indexed text plus a corroborating source — flagged where used.
Documentation conventions like the "golden thread" are practice/compliance norms, not scientific
findings, and are cited to a practitioner reference at orientation level and labeled as such._

## Tier: the clinical interview and the mental status exam

- **[S1] Voss, R. M., & M Das, J. (updated 2023). *Mental Status Examination.* StatPearls / NCBI Bookshelf, NBK546682.**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546682/
  _Peer-reviewed clinical reference. Authority: high for MSE domains and their definitions._
  The MSE covers **appearance, behavior, motor activity, speech, mood, affect, thought process,
  thought content, perceptions, cognition (alertness/orientation/attention/memory/abstract
  reasoning), insight, and judgment.** Key distinctions the page draws explicitly: **mood** is the
  patient's *subjective, self-reported* emotional state (documented in their own words) while
  **affect** is the clinician's *observed* emotional expression (euthymic, flat, labile, congruent/
  incongruent); **thought process** is the *form/organization* of thinking (circumstantial,
  tangential, flight of ideas, loose associations, perseveration, blocking) while **thought content**
  is *what* is thought about (delusions, obsessions, suicidal/homicidal ideation); **insight** is the
  patient's understanding of their illness while **judgment** is their capacity to make sound
  decisions — and the two dissociate (poor insight can coexist with fair judgment). The MSE is a
  *cross-sectional snapshot of current state*, "a subjective assessment … which may vary
  significantly between clinicians," and it does **not** by itself constitute a diagnosis — it aids
  diagnosis only combined with history, substance/medical history, and collateral/objective data.

- **[S7] Sommers-Flanagan, J. (2019). *The Clinical Interview as an Assessment Tool* (blog essay drawn from *Clinical Interviewing*, Sommers-Flanagan & Sommers-Flanagan). johnsommersflanagan.com.**
  https://johnsommersflanagan.com/2019/06/29/the-clinical-interview-as-an-assessment-tool/
  _The Unit's anchor author. Authority: high for interview structure and stance (the standard counseling text)._
  The clinical interview is the **foundational assessment method** — it organizes, assesses, and
  communicates information about a client's current state, and (for risk) is "the gold standard."
  **Four assessment-interview types:** (1) the **intake interview** (the most common; gathers initial
  information), (2) the **psychodiagnostic interview** (establishes a diagnosis to guide treatment),
  (3) the **mental status examination** (a semi-structured protocol across ~nine domains), and (4)
  **suicide assessment** (see Unit 8). The **intake** addresses three core areas: the **presenting
  problem**, the **psychosocial (biopsychosocial) history**, and **current functioning**. Structure
  ranges from **unstructured → semi-structured → highly structured**, and skilled clinicians blend
  structured elements into a relational frame — rapport and information-gathering are not opposed;
  the relationship is itself part of the assessment (ties back to [[unit01-helping-skills]]).

## Tier: screening instruments (validation primaries + corroboration)

- **[S2] Kroenke, K., Spitzer, R. L., & Williams, J. B. W. (2001). *The PHQ-9: Validity of a Brief Depression Severity Measure.* Journal of General Internal Medicine, 16(9), 606–613.**
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1046/j.1525-1497.2001.016009606.x
  _The PHQ-9 validation primary. Authority: high (the definitive source for the instrument)._
  The **PHQ-9** is a **9-item** self-report scoring each of the DSM major-depression symptoms 0–3
  ("not at all" → "nearly every day") over the last two weeks; **total 0–27**. Severity anchors:
  **5 / 10 / 15 / 20 = mild / moderate / moderately severe / severe** (0–4 minimal). At a cut-point
  of **≥10**, sensitivity and specificity for major depression are both **88%**. Internal
  consistency Cronbach's α = **0.86–0.89**; strong test-retest. **Item 9** screens passive/active
  thoughts of death or self-harm — a positive answer obligates a direct risk follow-up (Unit 8). The
  instrument maps one item to each of the nine MDD criteria, so it doubles as a criteria refresher.

- **[S3] Spitzer, R. L., Kroenke, K., Williams, J. B. W., & Löwe, B. (2006). *A Brief Measure for Assessing Generalized Anxiety Disorder: The GAD-7.* Archives of Internal Medicine, 166(10), 1092–1097.**
  https://ctc-ri.org/sites/default/files/uploads/gad7_ref.pdf
  _The GAD-7 validation primary. Authority: high._
  The **GAD-7** is a **7-item** self-report (0–3 each), **total 0–21**; cut-points **5 / 10 / 15 =
  mild / moderate / severe**. At **≥10**: sensitivity **89%**, specificity **82%** for GAD. Internal
  consistency α ≈ **0.92**; good test-retest and convergent validity. Though built for GAD, it also
  performs as a screen for **panic disorder** (sens ~74%), **social anxiety disorder** (~72%), and
  **PTSD** (~66%) — a reminder that a positive screen names a *domain of distress*, not a diagnosis.

- **[S6] American Psychological Association. *Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9 & PHQ-2).* APA assessment-tools resource.**
  https://www.apa.org/pi/about/publications/caregivers/practice-settings/assessment/tools/patient-health
  _Professional-body summary. Authority: high for practical use and access._
  Confirms PHQ-9 structure/scoring and that scores **≥10** carry a 7–13.6× increased likelihood of a
  depression diagnosis while **≤4** drops it below ~1-in-25. The **PHQ-2** (first two items — depressed
  mood + anhedonia) is an **ultra-brief pre-screen**; a positive PHQ-2 prompts the full PHQ-9. The
  PHQ family is **free to use** and available in 30+ languages (a real reason for its ubiquity).

- **[S10] Rutter, L. A., & Brown, T. A. (2017). *Psychometric Properties of the GAD-7 in a Heterogeneous Psychiatric Sample.* Frontiers in Psychology / J Psychopathol Behav Assess. PMC6691128.**
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6691128/
  _Peer-reviewed psychometric replication. Authority: high for reliability/validity numbers beyond primary-care samples._
  In a heterogeneous clinical sample the GAD-7 was **unidimensional** (one-factor), α = **0.83–0.93**,
  with strong convergent validity (BAI r = 0.69; SCL-90 anxiety r = 0.76) and sensitivity to
  treatment change (Cohen's d ≈ 0.5). Suggests a slightly lower optimal cut (**≥8**: sens 0.92,
  spec 0.70) in some populations — illustrating that cut-points are **population-dependent**, not
  universal constants.

## Tier: reliability, validity, and screening statistics

- **[S5] Institute of Medicine (US) Committee. *Overview of Psychological Testing.* In: Psychological Testing in the Service of Disability Determination. NCBI Bookshelf, NBK305233 (2015).**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305233/
  _National Academies / IOM consensus report. Authority: high for measurement theory._
  **Reliability** = the stability/consistency of scores: **test-retest** (across time), **inter-rater**
  (across scorers), **parallel/alternate forms**, and **internal consistency** (item homogeneity —
  Cronbach's α). **Validity** = "the degree to which evidence and theory support the interpretations
  of test scores for proposed uses" — and critically, **validity attaches to the interpretation/use,
  not to the test itself.** Evidence types: **content, criterion-related (predictive/concurrent),
  and construct.** The report states plainly that reliable scores **do not** guarantee validity.
  **Standardization** = fixed administration procedures plus a **representative normative sample**;
  applying a test outside its norming population risks misinterpretation. Trustworthy testing needs
  reliability + validity evidence + standardized administration + representative norms + qualified
  administration + fairness across groups — psychometrics alone don't license clinical use.

- **[S11] Price, P. C., Jhangiani, R., Chiang, I. A., et al. *Reliability and Validity of Measurement.* In: Research Methods in Psychology (2nd Canadian Ed.), open textbook (opentextbc.ca / KPU Pressbooks).**
  https://opentextbc.ca/researchmethods/chapter/reliability-and-validity-of-measurement/
  _Peer-reviewed open educational resource. Authority: high for orientation-level psychometrics (full text 403'd to automated fetch; content via search-indexed summary + corroboration by S5)._
  Reliability kinds — **test-retest** (same test, two times), **internal consistency** (split-half;
  Cronbach's α, ~**0.70+** acceptable), **inter-rater** (agreement across judges). Validity kinds —
  **face** (looks right), **content** (covers the whole construct), **criterion** (**concurrent** vs.
  **predictive**), **construct** (fits the theoretical network; **convergent** + **discriminant**).
  The load-bearing relationship: a measure can be **reliable but not valid** (consistently measuring
  the wrong thing), and **reliability is necessary but not sufficient** for validity — "you can't be
  valid without being reliable, but you can be reliable without being valid."

- **[S9] Shreffler, J., & Huecker, M. R. (updated 2023). *Diagnostic Testing Accuracy: Sensitivity, Specificity, Predictive Values and Likelihood Ratios.* StatPearls / NCBI Bookshelf, NBK557491.**
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557491/
  _Peer-reviewed clinical reference. Authority: high._
  **Sensitivity** = true positives ÷ all who have the condition (a *sensitive* test, when negative,
  helps rule OUT — "SnNOut"); **specificity** = true negatives ÷ all who don't (a *specific* test,
  when positive, helps rule IN — "SpPin"). The two **trade off**: pushing a cut-point to catch more
  cases raises sensitivity and lowers specificity. **PPV/NPV** (how much a positive/negative result
  means) depend on **prevalence** — the same test yields a lower PPV in a low-prevalence population.
  Screening instruments are deliberately tuned toward **high sensitivity** (miss few cases, accept
  false positives); a positive screen is a **flag to assess further, not a diagnosis**.

## Tier: case conceptualization and treatment planning

- **[S4] Kramer, G. A., & Rogers, J. R. (2023, exemplar). *Case Formulation and Intervention: Application of the Five Ps Framework in Substance Use Counseling.* The Professional Counselor (NBCC).**
  https://tpcjournal.nbcc.org/case-formulation-and-intervention-application-of-the-five-ps-framework-in-substance-use-counseling/
  _Peer-reviewed counseling journal. Authority: high for the counseling case-conceptualization angle._
  Case formulation (a.k.a. **conceptualization**) builds a **multidimensional, idiographic picture**
  of the client that "allows clinicians to target specific areas of concern" — Macneil et al. (2012)
  argued diagnosis alone is insufficient; you must add causal, lifestyle, and personal factors. The
  **Five Ps:** **Presenting problem**; **Predisposing** factors (biological/environmental/personality
  vulnerabilities that raise risk); **Precipitating** factors (proximal triggers of the current
  episode); **Perpetuating** factors (what sustains/reinforces the problem); **Protective** factors
  (strengths, relationships, resources that buffer). The framework "permits counselors to both assess
  and intervene essentially simultaneously" and — unlike CBT-bound formulation models — "does not
  adhere to any particular theoretical orientation," so the counselor's theory (Unit 2) still chooses
  the intervention.

- **[S8] PsychDB. *Biopsychosocial Model and Case Formulation.* psychdb.com (teaching reference).**
  https://www.psychdb.com/teaching/biopsychosocial-case-formulation
  _Clinician-teaching reference (orientation-tier). Authority: moderate — corroborates S4's Ps and crosses them with the biopsychosocial grid._
  Presents the **4 Ps × biopsychosocial grid**: each P (predisposing / precipitating / perpetuating /
  protective) is asked across the **biological, psychological, and social** domains (e.g., predisposing
  biological = genetics/temperament; predisposing social = early adversity; perpetuating psychological
  = maladaptive coping; protective social = supportive relationships). Frames formulation as the
  **explanatory** counterpart to diagnosis: "what is wrong, how it got that way, and what can be done
  about it," attributing the biopsychosocial frame to **Engel (1977/1980)** and its systematization to
  **Weerasekera (1993)**. (See [[concept-biopsychosocial-model]] from Unit 5.)

- **[S12] Fortney, J. C., Unützer, J., Wrenn, G., et al. (2017). *A Tipping Point for Measurement-Based Care.* Psychiatric Services, 68(2), 179–188.**
  https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/full/10.1176/appi.ps.201500439
  _Peer-reviewed policy/evidence statement. Authority: high (full PDF 403'd; core claims via the journal's abstract/indexed text)._
  **Measurement-based care (MBC)** = the routine administration of validated symptom scales (PHQ-9,
  GAD-7, PCL, AUDIT-C) at each visit, with the scores fed back to guide treatment decisions.
  MBC **improves outcomes** — effects are strongest for patients who would otherwise not improve —
  because it **detects non-response and deterioration earlier** than clinical impression alone
  (clinicians relying on unaided judgment routinely fail to notice worsening). It operationalizes the
  screening→monitoring loop: the same instrument that flags a problem at intake tracks whether
  treatment is working. Underused despite the evidence — the "tipping point" argument.

- **[S13] Doran, G. T. (1981). *There's a S.M.A.R.T. Way to Write Management's Goals and Objectives.* Management Review, 70(11), 35–36. (History via the SMART-criteria overview.)**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMART_criteria
  _Origin citation for the SMART acronym. Authority: high for provenance; the clinical adaptation is convention._
  SMART originated in **management**, not clinical work — Doran's original words were **Specific,
  Measurable, Assignable, Realistic, Time-related**; the counseling adaptation reads **Specific,
  Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.** It is a *format for writing objectives*, not a
  therapy — its value in treatment planning is forcing vague aims ("feel less anxious") into
  reviewable ones ("reduce GAD-7 from 16 to ≤7 within 12 weeks").

- **[S14] ICANotes / SonderMind. *Golden Thread Documentation for Behavioral Health.* (practitioner/compliance references.)**
  https://www.icanotes.com/2025/10/20/golden-thread-documentation-mental-health/
  _Practitioner/vendor references (orientation-tier). Authority: low-moderate — a documentation/compliance convention, not a scientific claim; cited as such._
  The **"golden thread"** is the auditable chain **assessment/diagnosis → treatment-plan goal →
  measurable objective → session interventions → progress notes → review**: any note should trace
  backward to the goal it serves and the diagnosis that makes it medically necessary. Payers reimburse
  for **impairment**, not symptoms, so the plan must tie the diagnosis to functional limitations and
  measurable targets. Distinguishes **goals** (broad clinical outcomes) from **objectives** (small,
  measurable steps toward them).

## Points of disagreement / uncertainty found (surfaced in readings)

1. **A screen is not a diagnosis — and cut-points are not laws.** The PHQ-9 and GAD-7 are tuned for
   **high sensitivity** to avoid missing cases, which means false positives are expected; a positive
   screen mandates a clinical interview, never a diagnosis by number. And the "≥10" cut-point is
   population-dependent (≥8 optimal in some psychiatric samples) — treat cut-scores as calibrated
   flags, not thresholds of truth. [S2][S3][S9][S10]
2. **Reliability ≠ validity, and neither licenses use.** A test can be perfectly consistent and
   consistently wrong; validity attaches to the *interpretation for a purpose*, and even a valid,
   reliable test misinterprets when applied outside its normative population — the culture-fair-
   assessment worry from [[unit04-multicultural]] lands here. [S5][S11]
3. **Diagnosis vs. formulation is the unit's spine.** Diagnosis (Unit 6) names *what*; case
   conceptualization explains *how it got this way and what maintains it* and points to *what to do* —
   filtered through the counselor's theory (Unit 2). The DSM label is an input to the formulation, not
   a substitute for it. [S4][S8]
4. **The MSE is a snapshot, and a subjective one.** It captures current state, varies between
   clinicians, and only supports (never makes) a diagnosis — over-reading a single MSE, or confusing
   mood with affect / process with content / insight with judgment, is the common beginner error.
   [S1]
5. **"Measurable" can crowd out "meaningful."** SMART goals and MBC genuinely catch non-response
   earlier and satisfy payers — but a plan built only from what a scale can score risks reducing the
   person to a number and importing the medical model's incentives (the same billing pressure flagged
   in [[concept-dsm-structure]]). Measure to serve the work, not the reverse. [S12][S13][S14]
6. **Provenance caveats.** PHQ-9/GAD-7 primaries and the StatPearls/IOM references were read
   directly; the Psychiatric Services MBC full text and the OER reliability chapter 403'd to automated
   access and are cited via abstract/indexed summaries plus a corroborating source. The golden-thread
   material is a documentation convention from practitioner references, labeled orientation-tier.
