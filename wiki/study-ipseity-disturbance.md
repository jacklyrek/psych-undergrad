# Schizophrenia as a Self-Disorder (the Ipseity-Disturbance Model)

**One line:** Sass and Parnas (2003) proposed that the core of schizophrenia is not hallucinations or delusions but a disturbance of **ipseity**, the most basic, pre-reflective sense of being a self: that *my* experiences are simply *mine* and that I am present in them. The **EASE** interview made these subtle "self-disorders" measurable. Two decades of data show they cluster in the schizophrenia spectrum and predict transition to psychosis. [S29][S30][S31]

This is the **most empirically developed** strand of phenomenological psychiatry, and the clearest example of phenomenology producing testable clinical science.

## The model

**Ipseity** (Latin *ipse*, "self") is the minimal self: the implicit, first-person "for-me-ness" of experience that normally goes entirely unnoticed. You don't have to *check* whether your thoughts are yours. [S29]

In the original 2003 paper, schizophrenia is a disturbance of ipseity with two complementary aspects: [S29]

| Component | What it is | What it can feel like |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyperreflexivity** | exaggerated self-consciousness: things normally tacit (bodily sensations, thoughts, the act of perceiving) become objects of explicit, alienated attention | "I have to watch myself think." "I notice my own seeing." |
| **Diminished self-affection** | a weakened sense of existing as the subject of one's experience; a thinned "I" | "I feel like I'm not really here." "My thoughts feel anonymous." |

The 2025 twenty-year update adds a third, interlocking component: [S31]
| **Disturbed grip (hold) on the world** | unstable immersion in the world; loss of the implicit sense of what's relevant and obvious | "Everything is equally important, or equally meaningless." (compare Blankenburg's *loss of natural self-evidence*, [[theory-phenomenological-psychopathology]]) |

**Why this is a new way to see psychosis:** on this model, many positive symptoms may grow *out of* self-disturbance. If one's own inner speech is hyperreflexively objectified and loses its felt "mineness," it becomes intelligible how it could come to be experienced as a voice, or as a thought inserted from outside. [S29] (This is the model's *proposal*, not an established mechanism.)

## The EASE (2005)

The **Examination of Anomalous Self-Experience** is a semi-structured, phenomenologically oriented interview covering domains such as cognition and stream of consciousness, self-awareness and presence, bodily experiences, demarcation (self/other boundary) and transitivism, and existential reorientation. [S30][S31] It lets clinicians and researchers **reliably** rate self-disorders that standard symptom interviews miss. [S31]

## The evidence (as summarized by the model's authors, 2025)
- **Specificity:** meta-analytic data show self-disorders **hyper-aggregate** in the schizophrenia spectrum, both clinical and subclinical (e.g., schizotypal), and **not** in other mental disorders. [S31]
- **Temporal priority and stability:** self-disorders often **precede** diagnosable psychotic symptoms and are stable over time. [S31]
- **Prediction:** in help-seeking young people, self-disorders **predict transition to psychosis**. [S31]

## Why this matters for a counselor
- **These are the experiences young people bring to a counselor before anyone thinks "psychosis."** "I feel unreal," "I watch myself from outside," "my thoughts don't feel like mine," "I don't get what's obvious to everyone else." They are often vague, hard to put into words, and easy to misread as anxiety, depersonalization, identity questioning, or ordinary adolescence.
- **What to do with that:** take such reports seriously, describe them carefully in the person's own words, and **refer for specialist (early-psychosis) assessment** when they persist or cluster. Don't diagnose, and don't dismiss ([[concept-scope-of-practice]], [[concept-psychotic-disorders]]).
- **Relational stance.** Hyperreflexive clients can be made *worse* by relentless introspective questioning. Grounding, concrete here-and-now contact, and not pushing endless self-analysis are reasonable cautions. (Clinical inference from the model, not a tested protocol.)

## What's contested
- **Written by proponents.** The main summaries come from the Copenhagen school that built the model. Weigh them accordingly. [S31]
- Open challenges the authors themselves acknowledge (raised by Feyaerts and Sass): how **specific** self-disorders really are; whether they are independent of **interpersonal and contextual** factors; and the lack of clear **neural correlates**. [S31]
- It is **not** a DSM criterion. DSM-5-TR schizophrenia is defined by the five symptom domains and duration ([[concept-psychotic-disorders]]). The ipseity model is a proposal about the experiential core beneath those criteria.
