# The Helping Professions Compared

**The idea:** four (plus) professions all "do therapy," but they differ in degree, training length,
license, professional identity, and — most concretely — **who can prescribe medication**. You need
this map to refer well, to coordinate care, and to explain your own role to clients (a genuine
informed-consent element, A.2.b). [S1][S7]

## The comparison table (learn this cold)

| | **Professional counselor (LPC/LMHC/LCMHC)** | **Clinical/counseling psychologist** | **Clinical social worker (LCSW)** | **Psychiatrist (MD/DO)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Degree | **master's** in counseling (~2–3 yrs; CACREP-accredited programs are the standard) | **doctorate** — PhD (research-heavy), PsyD (practice-heavy), or EdD (4–6 yrs + internship) | **master's** in social work (MSW, ~2 yrs) | **medical degree** + 3–4 yr psychiatry residency |
| Post-degree licensure path | ~2 yrs supervised practice + exam (e.g., NCE/NCMHCE) | 1–2 yrs supervised practice + EPPP exam | ~2–3 yrs supervised clinical work + exam | medical licensing + board certification |
| Signature lens | wellness, development, prevention — the person in their life context | psychological science: assessment, testing, diagnosis, therapy | **person-in-environment**: systems, resources, advocacy alongside therapy | biological/medical: brain, body, medication |
| Distinctive functions | counseling/psychotherapy; strong identity around the therapeutic relationship | **psychological testing/assessment** (IQ, personality, neuropsych) is largely their turf | therapy **plus** case management, connecting clients to concrete resources | **prescribing**; medical workups; hospitalization decisions |
| Psychotherapy? | yes | yes | yes | trained in it; in practice many focus on medication management |
| Prescribes? | **no** (no state) | **no**, except specially trained psychologists in a **handful of states** (see below) | **no** | **yes** |

Adjacent roles worth recognizing: **psychiatric-mental-health nurse practitioners (PMHNPs)**
prescribe in every state and provide much of real-world medication management; **marriage & family therapists (LMFTs)** are master's-level clinicians with a systems specialty; **school counselors**
hold a distinct master's credential; "**psychotherapist**" is, in most states, not a protected title
by itself — the license behind it is what matters. [S7]

## The prescribing exception (RxP) — and why the number keeps changing

Psychologists with a **postdoctoral master's in clinical psychopharmacology**, supervised practice
hours, and a national psychopharmacology exam can prescribe in a small set of states: **New Mexico (2002, first), Louisiana (2004), Illinois (2014), Iowa (2016), Idaho (2017)** — APA's page stops there, but **Colorado (2023)** and **Utah (2024)** have since joined (≈7 states as of 2024–25, a
moving target as bills advance elsewhere). Appropriately trained psychologists may also prescribe
in the **military (DoD)**, the **U.S. Public Health Service**, and the **Indian Health Service**. [S8]

For everyone else, the answer to "can you give me something for this?" is a referral — typically to
a psychiatrist, PMHNP, or the client's primary-care physician (who in fact write the majority of
psychotropic prescriptions in the U.S.). Coordinating with prescribers is a core counselor skill
(Unit 11 covers the medications themselves). [S7][S8]

## Counselor professional identity (the CACREP angle)

"Professional counseling orientation" is a CACREP core area partly *because* counseling had to
carve an identity distinct from psychology and social work. The distinguishing story programs
teach: counseling grew from the **guidance/vocational movement** (Frank Parsons, early 1900s) and
the **humanistic tradition** ([[theory-person-centered]]), so its center of gravity is **developmental and wellness-oriented** — clients are people navigating problems of living, not primarily patients with pathology. In practice the professions' day-to-day work overlaps heavily (all four diagnose and treat in most settings); identity shows up in emphasis, training, and advocacy priorities more than in what the hour with a client looks like. [S7]

## Choosing a referral (the applied skill)

- Needs medication evaluation → **psychiatrist / PMHNP** (or PCP for straightforward cases).
- Needs IQ, learning-disability, ADHD, or neuropsychological **testing** → **psychologist**.
- Needs housing, benefits, elder services, discharge planning *alongside* therapy → **LCSW**.
- Needs weekly psychotherapy for anxiety/depression/adjustment → any of the therapy professions; fit and availability matter more than the letters ([[theory-common-factors]]).

## What's contested (don't over-learn it)

- **RxP itself is contested**: organized medicine (and some psychologists) oppose psychologist
  prescribing on safety/training grounds; proponents cite rural access gaps and the military's
  decades of safe experience. Expect the state list to keep changing. [S8]
- **Turf boundaries are legislative, not scientific** — who may test, diagnose, or take insurance
  varies by state and payer, and the borders move under lobbying pressure. Treat any tidy
  "counselors do X, psychologists do Y" as a *tendency*, not a law of nature. [S7][S8]
