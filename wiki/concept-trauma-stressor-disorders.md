# Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders

This chapter is unique in the DSM: every diagnosis in it **requires an identified external event**. Nowhere else does etiology sit inside the criteria. That gives the whole family a two-part grammar — *which stressor* (a Criterion-A trauma, any stressor, or a death) and *which clock* (how long since, how long lasting). Master the grammar and the four diagnoses sort themselves.

⚠ *This page borders Unit 8's territory (trauma-informed care, crisis). Here we do the diagnostic map only; the neurobiology, treatment, and how-not-to-retraumatize belong to Unit 8.*

## The gate: Criterion A

PTSD and acute stress disorder open with a strict gate: exposure to **"actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence"** — by directly experiencing it, witnessing it in person, learning it happened to a close family member or friend, or repeated/extreme indirect exposure to aversive details in professional roles (first responders, forensic examiners). [S9] Ordinary media exposure doesn't qualify; neither do painful-but-nonviolent events (divorce, job loss) — those route to adjustment disorder. The gate is deliberately narrow so "trauma" keeps a clinical meaning; note that this is contested terrain (many clinicians argue chronic humiliation, racism, or emotional abuse can be traumatogenic without meeting Criterion A — the criteria are a line drawn, not a fact of nature).

## PTSD — the four-cluster architecture

The third of the syllabus's "big three." After a Criterion-A exposure, four symptom clusters, all required, **lasting >1 month** with distress/impairment [S9]:

| Cluster | Need | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B — Intrusion** | 1+ | intrusive memories, nightmares, flashbacks, intense distress/physiological reactivity at reminders |
| **C — Avoidance** | 1+ | of internal reminders (thoughts, feelings) and/or external cues (people, places, activities) |
| **D — Negative alterations in cognitions & mood** | 2+ | can't recall aspects of the event; "I am bad / the world is entirely dangerous"; distorted self-blame; persistent fear, guilt, shame; detachment; diminished interest; can't feel positive emotion |
| **E — Arousal & reactivity** | 2+ | irritability/aggression, recklessness/self-destructiveness, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, poor concentration, sleep disturbance |

Two specifiers worth knowing: **dissociative subtype** (prominent depersonalization/derealization) and **delayed expression** (full criteria not met until ≥6 months post-event). Lifetime prevalence ~6.1–9.2% in the U.S. — most people exposed to trauma do *not* develop PTSD; risk rises with dose, prior adversity, and peritraumatic response. [S9] That base-rate fact is clinically loaded both ways: don't assume trauma means PTSD, and don't miss it when it's there.

## The clock diagnoses around PTSD

- **Acute stress disorder:** same Criterion-A gate, similar symptom pool — but the window is **3 days to 1 month** post-event. Cross the month line still symptomatic and the diagnosis converts to PTSD. [S9]
- **Adjustment disorder:** emotional/behavioral symptoms **within 3 months of *any* identifiable stressor** (breakup, layoff, diagnosis, move) that are out of proportion or impairing, and that resolve **within 6 months of the stressor (or its consequences) ending**. It's the workhorse diagnosis for real suffering after non-Criterion-A events — and a chronically under-respected one; it's often what honest diagnosis looks like instead of stretching MDD or PTSD to fit. [S9]
- **Prolonged grief disorder (PGD):** added in DSM-5-TR. After the death of someone close — **≥12 months** prior (6 in children) — intense yearning for or preoccupation with the deceased, most days, plus grief symptoms (identity disruption, disbelief, avoidance of reminders, intense pain, numbness, meaninglessness, loneliness) causing impairment, **beyond cultural norms**. [S2] The support-side treatment of grief — including why *normal* grief must not be pathologized — is the PFA elective's [[concept-grief-models]]; the grief-vs-depression discrimination table is on [[concept-mood-disorders]].

**The sorting rule of thumb:** Criterion-A event + <1 month = acute stress disorder; + >1 month = PTSD. Non-Criterion-A stressor = adjustment disorder. Death + a year of yearning-centered impairment = PGD.

## Differentials that snag beginners

- **PTSD vs. adjustment disorder:** severity isn't the line — the *stressor type* is.
- **PTSD vs. [[concept-anxiety-disorders | panic disorder]]:** panic attacks cued by trauma reminders belong to PTSD; unexpected attacks with fear-of-the-fear are panic disorder.
- **PTSD vs. [[concept-ocd | OCD]]:** intrusions after trauma *replay something that happened*; obsessions are feared possibilities, usually recognized as products of one's own mind.
- **Flashback vs. hallucination:** a flashback is a dissociative *re-experiencing* of a real event; a hallucination ([[concept-psychotic-disorders]]) is a perception without any external source.

## In the room

Two Unit-8 previews that can't wait: **screening is asking, plainly and without pressure** — "Has anything happened to you that still intrudes on your life?" — and **detailed disclosure is not required for diagnosis or safety**; pushing for the full narrative before stabilization is how helpers re-traumatize. The counselor's Criterion-A footnote applies to you too: repeated professional exposure is a real risk channel (vicarious trauma — an ethical competency in Unit 8, previewed in [[concept-scope-of-practice]]'s impairment material).
