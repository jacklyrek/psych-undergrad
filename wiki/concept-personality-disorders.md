# Personality Disorders

Everything else in this unit is an *episode* — something that arrives, runs a course, and can remit. A personality disorder is different in kind: "an enduring pattern of inner experience and behavior that deviates significantly from the norms and expectations of the surrounding culture" — **pervasive** (across contexts), **inflexible**, **stable**, with onset traceable to adolescence or early adulthood, causing distress or impairment. [S11] The pattern shows up in cognition, affectivity, interpersonal functioning, and impulse control. Prevalence ~6.1% of the general population — far higher in clinical settings. [S11]

That "enduring pattern vs. episode" line is the first differential everywhere in this unit: two weeks of low mood is an episode ([[concept-mood-disorders]]); a lifelong style of stormy relationships is a pattern. It's also why the *culture clause* matters — the pattern must deviate from *the person's own culture's* expectations, not from yours. [S11]

## The three clusters and the ten disorders

| Cluster | Flavor | Disorders (one-line handle) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — odd / eccentric** | psychosis-adjacent, detached | **Paranoid** (pervasive distrust; others' motives read as malevolent) · **Schizoid** (genuine detachment from and disinterest in relationships, restricted affect) · **Schizotypal** (social deficits + cognitive-perceptual distortions: magical thinking, ideas of reference, odd speech) |
| **B — dramatic / emotional / erratic** | affect and impulse run hot | **Antisocial** (disregard for and violation of others' rights; deceit, aggression, lack of remorse; ≥18 with conduct disorder before 15) · **Borderline** (instability of relationships, self-image, and affect; frantic avoidance of abandonment; recurrent self-harm/suicidality) · **Histrionic** (excessive emotionality and attention-seeking) · **Narcissistic** (grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy) |
| **C — anxious / fearful** | anxiety-adjacent, controlled | **Avoidant** (social inhibition from felt inadequacy and hypersensitivity to negative evaluation — *wants* connection) · **Dependent** (submissive clinging, need to be taken care of) · **Obsessive-compulsive (OCPD)** (perfectionism and rigid control, ego-syntonic — see [[concept-ocd]] for the OCD contrast) |

Cluster-internal discriminations worth drilling: **schizoid vs. avoidant** (indifference to relationships vs. painful longing for them); **schizotypal vs. schizophrenia** (oddity without sustained psychosis — see [[concept-psychotic-disorders]]); **borderline vs. bipolar** (mood shifts over hours, interpersonally triggered, vs. episodes over weeks with changed energy/sleep — [[concept-mood-disorders]]); **narcissistic vs. antisocial** (grandiosity seeking admiration vs. exploitation without remorse).

## Treatment honesty

"With the exception of borderline personality disorder, there are no high-quality, evidence-based psychotherapies for personality disorders." [S11] The borderline exception is a genuine success story: **dialectical behavior therapy (DBT)** — skills for emotion regulation, distress tolerance, and relationships — has real trial support ([[concept-dbt-skills]] covers the modality, Unit 9). The clinical posture for the rest is long-game: alliance, consistency, and boundaries, not cure-by-protocol.

## Held carefully: stigma, countertransference, and the label

No diagnoses are more stigmatized *inside* the professions than these — "borderline" gets used in staff rooms as an insult meaning "difficult." Three disciplines keep you honest:

- **Diagnose the pattern, not the moment.** A person in crisis can look borderline; a person in a custody fight can look paranoid. The criteria require a *longitudinal*, cross-context pattern — which an intake cannot see. Be slow. [S11]
- **Countertransference is diagnostic data, not a verdict.** These patterns *pull* on the clinician — rescue fantasies, dread, anger. Noticing the pull ("I feel worked on") is useful; acting from it isn't. [S11] Your own attachment pattern shapes what pulls you ([[concept-attachment-styles]]).
- **Development matters.** Borderline features in particular correlate with disorganized attachment and early adversity ([[theory-attachment]]) — "risk, not destiny" applies, and it re-frames the behavior as an old survival strategy rather than manipulation.

## The alternative model (AMPD) — the dimensional future, maybe

The DSM-5-TR carries, in its "emerging measures" section, an **Alternative Model of Personality Disorders**: instead of ten categories, rate (1) *level of impairment* in personality functioning (self: identity, self-direction; interpersonal: empathy, intimacy) and (2) *pathological traits* across five domains (negative affectivity, detachment, antagonism, disinhibition, psychoticism). [S11] It exists because the categorical PDs are the manual's most embarrassing corner — massive overlap, heterogeneity, arbitrary thresholds — making personality the beachhead of the whole [[concept-categorical-vs-dimensional]] debate.
