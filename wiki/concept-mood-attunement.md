# Mood as Attunement

**One line:** in the phenomenological view, a **mood is not an inner feeling layered on top of a neutral world**. It is the background way the world *as a whole* shows up as mattering, and in which directions. Change the mood and you change which possibilities the world seems to offer. [S3][S34]

## Heidegger: *Befindlichkeit*

Heidegger's term *Befindlichkeit* (translated "state-of-mind," "attunement," "disposedness," "situatedness"; literally something like "how one finds oneself") names a structural feature of being-in-the-world ([[concept-being-in-the-world]]). [S3]

- **We are always in some mood**, even when it is the flat, unremarkable mood of an ordinary Tuesday.
- **Moods disclose.** Only because we are attuned can anything *matter* to us: be threatening, inviting, boring, beautiful. A world with no mood would not be neutral; it would be unintelligible. [S3]
- **Moods are neither subjective nor objective.** They "arise from being-in-the-world itself." They are not *in* me like a stomachache or *in* the room like its temperature. [S3]
- **Anxiety** is the special mood in which everyday meanings go flat, and Dasein faces its own existence ([[concept-being-in-the-world]]). [S3]

## Ratcliffe: existential feelings

Matthew Ratcliffe updates the idea for contemporary philosophy of psychiatry with the concept of **existential feelings**. [S34][S33]

- These are pre-intentional, bodily feelings that give us our **sense of belonging to the world** and **what kinds of possibility it contains**: feeling at home, feeling estranged, feeling that things are real, feeling that anything could happen.
- They sit **beneath** ordinary emotions. Emotions are about specific things (angry *at* him); existential feelings shape the whole field in which things can be encountered at all. [S34]
- Severe **depression** is, on this account, above all an alteration of existential feeling: the world is still perceived, but it no longer offers significant possibilities. One person Ratcliffe quotes says everything "looks the same yet is experienced completely different." [S33][S34] Full treatment: [[concept-phenomenology-of-depression]].

## Why this matters for counseling

- **Listen for the world-description, not only the feeling-word.** "I feel depressed" is the label. "Everything is behind glass," "nothing is for me anymore," "the world looks cardboard" describe the attunement. Reflecting *that* ("it's like the world has gone flat and far away") often lands more accurately than reflecting the label ([[concept-oars]], [[concept-empathy]]).
- **Mood vs. affect on the MSE.** The MSE records **mood** in the client's own words and **affect** as observed ([[concept-mental-status-exam]]). The phenomenological sense of "mood" is richer than either. It is the tone of the whole lived world. Don't confuse the MSE's technical use with Heidegger's. (Keep-apart note.)
- **Moods are not argued away.** A mood is a way the world is disclosed, not a belief, so it isn't refuted by evidence the way a thought might be. That fits the clinical experience that attunements usually shift through *action, relationship, and time* more than through argument ([[concept-behavioral-activation]]).

## Keep apart
| Term | Directed at | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Emotion** | a specific object | afraid *of the dog* |
| **Mood / attunement** | the world as a whole | everything seems menacing today |
| **Existential feeling** (Ratcliffe) | one's overall *belonging to* the world and its possibility-space | feeling unreal, cut off, as if nothing could ever matter again |

## What's contested
- Whether existential feelings are a distinct category or just intense moods is debated. Some critics argue depression is better described as a "de-situatedness" than a change of feeling. Fernandez's reply to Ratcliffe distinguishes the *structure* of being-situated from its *modes*. [S50]
- These are descriptions, not diagnostic tests.
