# Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)

**Why he matters:** Husserl **founded phenomenology** as a rigorous method for describing experience from the first-person point of view. Every other figure in this module is either his student, his critic, or both. [S1][S2]

## Life, briefly
- Trained first as a **mathematician** before turning to philosophy under **Franz Brentano** in Vienna, from whom he took the concept of **intentionality**. [S1][S2]
- Taught at Halle, Göttingen, and Freiburg. **Martin Heidegger** was his assistant and chosen successor at Freiburg; **Edith Stein** was an earlier assistant. [S2]
- Of Jewish origin (baptized Lutheran). Under the Nazi regime he was stripped of his university privileges and died in 1938. His enormous shorthand *Nachlass* (tens of thousands of pages) was smuggled out of Germany to Leuven, Belgium, where the Husserl Archives still edit it. (Standard biographical facts; see [S2].)

## How he differs from the others
- **vs. Heidegger:** Husserl starts from **consciousness**, reached by bracketing the world. Heidegger starts from **being-in-the-world**, which can't be bracketed. [S1][S3]
- **vs. Merleau-Ponty:** Husserl's subject is a transcendental consciousness. Merleau-Ponty's is a **lived body**. [S1][S4]
- **vs. psychology:** Husserl insisted phenomenology was *not* empirical psychology. Most "phenomenological" work in counseling is a looser borrowing of his *attitude* (describe before explaining). [S1][S39]
