# Ivey's Microskills Hierarchy

Allen Ivey's contribution (microcounseling, ~1967–71) was to take the vague instruction "build
rapport" and break it into **discrete, teachable, observable skills** arranged as a pyramid — you
master the lower ones before the higher ones do any good. The model has been studied extensively. [S5][S6]

## The ladder (base → top)
1. **Foundation — the core conditions + ethics + cultural competence.** Genuineness, empathy, UPR ([[concept-core-conditions]]) are the ground the whole pyramid stands on. [S5]
2. **Attending behavior.** The physical basics of being present: eye contact, vocal qualities
   (tone, pace), verbal tracking (following the client's topic, not yours), and open body language.
   This is "the foundation skill" — clients talk more when attended to. Treated in depth (SOLER, the
   three Vs + B, the use of silence) on [[concept-attending-and-listening]]. [S5]
3. **Observation skills.** Noticing *how* the client communicates — especially mismatches between
   words and nonverbals (saying "I'm fine" while tearing up). [S5]
4. **Questions.** Open questions ("What was that like?") to expand, closed questions ("How many?")
   to pin down specifics. Beginners over-rely on questions; used too much they turn a session into an interrogation. [S5]
5. **Reflecting skills** — **encouragers/minimal encouragers, paraphrasing, reflecting feeling,
   summarizing.** The workhorse listening skills (see the distinctions below). [S5][S7]
6. **Influencing skills** (top, higher-risk) — **interpretation**, self-disclosure, confrontation,
   directives. Powerful, but they impose the counselor's frame, so they require the lower skills to
   be solid first. [S5][S7]

## From skills to a session: Ivey's five-stage interview
The microskills are the *notes*; Ivey also gave a *structure* for a whole session — a five-stage arc that any theoretical orientation can fill in. It's a useful map for "what do I actually do for 50 minutes?" [S5]

1. **Empathic relationship** — build rapport and structure ("Hello — here's how we'll work today").
2. **Story and strengths** — draw out the client's concern *and* their resources (not just the problem).
3. **Goals** — establish what the client wants ("What would you like to happen?").
4. **Restory** — help the client generate new ways of thinking about and narrating the situation.
5. **Action** — move toward change the client will carry outside the room.

Notice the sequence: **relationship and story come before goals and action** — the same discipline as the stance note below, built into the shape of the session.

## The distinction that trips everyone up: reflect vs. paraphrase vs. interpret
These look similar and are constantly confused. The difference is **how far you go beyond the
client's words.** [S7]

| Skill | What you do | Stays inside the client's frame? |
|---|---|---|
| **Paraphrase (reflect content)** | Restate the *content* of what they said in fresh words | Yes — mirrors their meaning |
| **Reflect feeling** | Name the *emotion* underneath, matched to its intensity | Yes — surfaces what's implicit but present |
| **Summarize** | Condense several paraphrases/reflections; used at start/end | Yes — broadest of the three |
| **Interpret** | Add *your* read of meaning, cause, or need — beyond what they said | **No — you leave their frame** |

- **Paraphrase** ("So the deadline got moved up and now the whole plan is in question") — content,
  no new meaning added.
- **Reflection of feeling** ("It sounds like that left you anxious and a little betrayed") — names
  emotion the client implied but didn't label. Requires a wide feeling vocabulary. [S7]
- **Interpretation** ("I wonder if this keeps happening because you say yes before you've checked
  what you can handle") — introduces an explanation the client didn't offer. It's an *influencing*
  skill: it can deepen a session or, if premature, feel presumptuous. Earn it with the lower skills
  first. [S7]

## The stance payoff
The single most useful move in this unit is **reflect feeling, then go quiet.** A reflection invites
the client to say more; a question or an interpretation steers them toward *your* agenda. If your
instinct is to fix, default down the ladder to a reflection and let silence do the rest. (See
[[unit01-helping-skills]] stance note.)
