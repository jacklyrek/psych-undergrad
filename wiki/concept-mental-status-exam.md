# The Mental Status Examination (MSE)

The MSE is to psychiatry what the physical exam is to medicine: a **structured, cross-sectional description of the client's current state**, written in a shared vocabulary so another clinician reading your note sees roughly what you saw. Two things it is *not*: it is **not a diagnosis** (it describes present state; it only *supports* a diagnosis when combined with history, substance/medical history, and collateral data), and it is **not objective** — it is "a subjective assessment … which may vary significantly between clinicians based on their observation skills" [S1]. The discipline is to **describe, not interpret**: "tearful, avoided eye contact, spoke slowly" is an observation; "depressed" is a conclusion the MSE feeds but does not equal.

Most of the MSE is gathered by **watching and listening** during the interview — you're doing it whether or not you call it that — with only a few items (orientation, memory, attention, abstract reasoning) requiring you to *ask* specific questions.

## The domains

A common ordering (memory hooks vary — some use "ASEPTIC" or similar; the content matters more than the mnemonic) [S1]:

| Domain | What it captures | Observed or asked? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Appearance** | apparent age, dress, grooming, hygiene, marks (scars, tattoos) | observed |
| **Behavior / attitude** | cooperativeness, eye contact, distress, redirectability, psychomotor agitation/retardation | observed |
| **Motor activity** | gait, posture, tremor, tics, abnormal movements | observed |
| **Speech** | rate, rhythm, volume, fluency (pressured? slurred? impoverished?) | observed |
| **Mood** | the client's *self-reported* emotional state | asked (verbatim) |
| **Affect** | the *observed* emotional expression | observed |
| **Thought process** | the *form* of thinking — how thoughts connect | observed (inferred from speech) |
| **Thought content** | *what* is thought about — themes, preoccupations, ideation | observed / asked |
| **Perception** | hallucinations, illusions, depersonalization/derealization | observed / asked |
| **Cognition** | alertness, orientation, attention, memory, abstract reasoning | asked / tested |
| **Insight** | the client's understanding of their own condition | inferred / asked |
| **Judgment** | capacity to make sound decisions | inferred / asked |

## The three distinctions beginners flatten

These are the MSE's confusable pairs — and the reason it has its own cluster.

### Mood vs. affect

**Mood is what the client tells you; affect is what you see.** [S1]

- **Mood** — subjective, self-reported, documented in the client's own words: "I feel empty," "fine, I guess." It's the *client's* report of their emotional weather.
- **Affect** — the clinician's observation of expressed emotion, described on several axes: *quality* (euthymic, dysphoric, euphoric, anxious), *range* (full, constricted, blunted, **flat**), *stability* (**labile** = rapidly shifting), and *congruence* with mood and content.
- The **mismatch is the signal.** A client who says "I'm doing great" (mood) while tearful and trembling (affect) shows **incongruent** affect — and that gap is often more diagnostic than either alone. Flat affect points toward psychosis or severe depression; labile affect toward mania or intoxication.

Memory hook: *mood is the climate (what they report over time), affect is the weather right now (what you observe).*

### Thought process vs. thought content

**Process is the *shape* of thinking; content is the *subject*.** [S1] They dissociate completely — you can have a perfectly organized thought process delivering a floridly delusional content, and vice versa.

- **Thought process** (form) — the connections between ideas: *linear/goal-directed* (normal), **circumstantial** (over-detailed but eventually returns to the point), **tangential** (wanders off and never returns), **flight of ideas** (rapid, loosely connected — mania), **loose associations** (no logical link — psychosis), **perseveration** (stuck on one idea), **thought blocking** (abrupt stops).
- **Thought content** (subject) — what the thinking is *about*: **delusions** (fixed false beliefs — paranoid, grandiose, somatic, thought insertion/broadcasting), **obsessions**, **phobias**, and — always assessed — **suicidal and homicidal ideation** (passive vs. active; plan; intent; means).

### Insight vs. judgment

**Insight is understanding the illness; judgment is deciding well about it.** [S1] They also dissociate — a subtle, exam-favorite point.

- **Insight** — does the client recognize they have a condition, and grasp its nature? Rated poor / limited / fair / good. Example: fair insight = knowing the voices aren't real; poor insight = denying obvious symptoms.
- **Judgment** — can the client make sound, safe decisions and anticipate consequences? Assessed from history and observed choices (the old "what would you do if you smelled smoke in a theater?" is crude and largely abandoned in favor of real-life decisions).
- The dissociation: a person with **poor insight** ("I'm not ill") can still show **fair judgment** (takes the medication anyway, to avoid another hospitalization) — and someone with good insight can repeatedly make dangerous choices. Don't collapse the two.

## Cognition, briefly

Screened, not exhaustively tested, in a counseling intake: **alertness** (alert → drowsy → stupor), **orientation** (person / place / time / situation — acute disorientation suggests **delirium** or intoxication, an easily-missed medical emergency, not primary psychiatric illness), **attention** (serial 7s, spelling "world" backward), **memory** (immediate / recent / remote), and **abstract reasoning** (similarities, proverbs — concrete interpretations flag psychosis or neurocognitive impairment). Suspected cognitive impairment routes to a fuller instrument (MoCA, MMSE, Mini-Cog) and a medical referral, not a counseling formulation.

## Why counselors document it

- **A shared, portable snapshot.** Your MSE lets the next provider — and future-you — see the baseline and detect change. It is the natural companion to the [[concept-screening-tools | severity scores]] and to [[concept-treatment-planning | measurement-based care]].
- **A change detector.** "Affect brighter, thought process now linear, no longer responding to internal stimuli" is progress you can point to.
- **Read it against the baseline.** An MSE means little without the developmental and cultural norm it's measured against — apparent guardedness can be rational ([[unit04-multicultural]]), and what's odd at one age is ordinary at another ([[unit05-development]]). Describe; don't pathologize the difference.
