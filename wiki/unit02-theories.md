# Unit 2 — Counseling Theories: The Major Schools

> **The one-line version:** your theory is the **lens** that tells you what to pay attention to and
> what to do next. Each major school answers two questions differently — *why do people get stuck?*
> and *how do they get better?* You don't have to commit to one yet. You need a map so you don't feel lost mid-session. [S5]

This unit surveys six families of theory. Read each page through one filter — the syllabus's practice
rep, which is also the spine of the whole unit:

> **"This theory says people get stuck because ___, and they get better by ___."**

If you can fill in that sentence for all six, you've done the core work of Unit 2.

## The map (read in this order; they build on each other historically)

1. **[[theory-psychodynamic]]** — Freud and his descendants. The **unconscious**, **defenses**, and
   **transference**. Stuck because of buried, unresolved early conflict; better by making the
   unconscious conscious (insight). The historical root the others react against. [S1]
2. **[[theory-person-centered]]** — Rogers' humanistic approach. The **actualizing tendency** and the
   **client as expert.** Stuck because the drive to grow got blocked by conditions of worth; better
   by a relationship offering the core conditions. **Builds directly on Unit 1.** [S3]
3. **[[theory-existential]]** — not a technique set but a stance on the human condition. Stuck because
   of how we avoid the **givens of existence** (death, freedom, isolation, meaning); better by facing
   them and choosing responsibly. [S6][S7]
4. **[[theory-cbt]]** — Beck and Ellis. **Thoughts → feelings → behavior.** Stuck because of
   distorted thinking and learned behavior; better by testing and changing both. The most-researched, most-requested family. [S4][S9]
5. **[[theory-family-systems]]** — Bowen and Minuchin. The problem lives **in the relationships, not
   the person.** Stuck because of dysfunctional patterns in the system; better by changing the
   patterns. [S10][S11]
6. **[[theory-postmodern]]** — solution-focused and narrative. There is **no single objective truth**;
   reality is **constructed in language.** Stuck inside a problem-saturated story; better by
   re-authoring it. The therapist gives up the expert chair. [S13][S14]

## The comparison that does the work

The point of the unit is not six isolated summaries — it's being able to tell them apart. This table is
the interleaving target (`cluster: theory-families`):

| Family              | Stuck because…                                                                 | Better by…                                                                                     | Time focus                           | Therapist stance                               |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Psychodynamic**   | unconscious unresolved early conflict; defenses [S1]                           | insight — making the unconscious conscious; working through transference [S1]                  | **past**                             | interpretive, expert [S5]                      |
| **Person-centered** | the actualizing tendency was blocked by conditions of worth; incongruence [S3] | a genuine relationship (empathy, UPR, congruence) that frees growth [S3]                       | present/future                       | **non-directive**, follows the client [S5]     |
| **Existential**     | avoiding the givens of existence; an existential vacuum [S6][S7]               | facing the givens; choosing meaning and taking responsibility [S6][S7]                         | present, whole-life                  | a fellow traveler; philosophical [S7]          |
| **CBT**             | distorted thoughts + maladaptive learned behavior [S4]                         | identifying/testing thoughts; changing behavior (restructuring, exposure, activation) [S4][S5] | **present**                          | directive, teaching, collaborative [S5]        |
| **Family systems**  | dysfunctional patterns in the relationship system (not the individual) [S10]   | changing the patterns/structure; differentiating self [S10][S11]                               | present patterns + multigenerational | active, sometimes director of enactments [S10] |
| **Postmodern**      | being trapped in a problem-saturated dominant story; no fixed truth [S13]      | re-authoring the story; building on exceptions and strengths [S13][S14]                        | present/future                       | **non-expert**, collaborative, curious [S13]   |

A useful coarse grouping: the **first three** (psychodynamic, person-centered, existential) are
*insight/relationship*-oriented and grew out of European depth psychology and philosophy; **CBT** is
*action/learning*-oriented and empirical; **family systems** moves the unit of analysis from the
person to the system; **postmodern** challenges the idea that the counselor knows the truth at all.

## The stance note

Two common traps here:
- **Picking a theory for its elegance, then defending it.** Theory is a tool
  for *attention*, not an identity. Hold it loosely.
- **Gravitating straight to CBT because it's the most structured.** CBT *is* the most protocol-like and
  can feel like the most comfortable place to start — which is exactly why you should make yourself understand the relational and meaning-based schools, where the "fix-it" reflex (see [[unit01-helping-skills]]) actively backfires. Person-centered and existential therapy work *because* the counselor resists solving.

## Where most counselors actually land: integrative

Very few experienced clinicians use a single pure school. Across large surveys, **integrative/eclectic**
is consistently among the most common self-identified orientations (estimates vary widely by sample — on the order of one in four or more). [S15] The mature move is to learn each lens well enough to choose deliberately, then combine them in a principled way — not to grab techniques at random. You are not expected to commit yet; you're expected to be able to *think in* each one.

## What's genuinely contested (so you don't over-learn it)
- **Psychodynamic constructs are hard to falsify.** The unconscious and transference are clinically
  useful but resist clean measurement; the evidence base is solid for depression/anxiety, weaker for OCD/PTSD/psychosis. [S1] — see [[theory-psychodynamic]].
- **"Client as expert" can be overstated.** Person-centered conditions are widely held to be
  necessary but **not sufficient** for many clients (Unit 1). [S3] — see [[theory-person-centered]].
- **Differentiation of self** (Bowen) is central but its measurement and cross-cultural validity are
  debated. [S11][S12] — see [[theory-family-systems]].
- **"No single truth"** (postmodern) is philosophically contested and can sit awkwardly next to
  evidence-based practice; in practice most clinicians treat it as a *stance toward the client's
  meaning*, not a denial of clinical reality. [S13]
