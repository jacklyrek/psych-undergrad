# The Therapeutic Alliance

The "alliance" (or *working alliance*) is the collaborative relationship between counselor and
client. Edward **Bordin (1979)** gave it the model everyone still uses, and his key move was making
it **transtheoretical** — the same three components describe the working relationship in *any*
orientation, from psychodynamic to CBT. [S4]

## Bordin's three components
- **Bond** — the affective relationship: trust, liking, respect, the sense of being a team. This is
  what the core conditions and microskills directly build. [S4]
- **Goal** — agreement on *what you're working toward.* The client and counselor share the
  destination rather than the counselor pursuing their own. [S4]
- **Task** — agreement on *the in-session activities and each person's role* — what you'll actually
  do together to get there, and that the client believes it will help. [S4]

A useful way to hold it: **bond** is the relationship, **goal** is the *where*, **task** is the
*how*. A rupture in any of the three weakens the alliance, and they're somewhat independent — you can
have warm rapport (bond) while quietly disagreeing about goals or tasks, which stalls therapy.

## How Bordin thought it works
Bordin argued the alliance influences outcome **not because the relationship is curative on its own**,
but because it's the ingredient that lets the client **accept, follow, and believe in** the
treatment. The bond makes the work possible; the work does the changing. This is the bridge between "be warm" and "do effective therapy." [S4]

## The evidence
The alliance is one of the most robust predictors of outcome in all of psychotherapy research:
**r ≈ .28 across 295 studies and 30,000+ patients** (Flückiger et al., 2018), holding across
treatment types. [S2] Bordin's operationalization is what made it *measurable* — it spawned the
**Working Alliance Inventory (WAI)** and similar instruments. [S4] (See [[theory-common-factors]] for
the full evidence discussion and the correlation-vs-causation caveat.)

## Why it matters in the room
Bond/goal/task is a fast diagnostic when a session feels stuck. Ask: is the *bond* frayed, or do we
disagree on the *goal*, or on the *task*? Each points to a different repair. The skill of noticing
and repairing **alliance ruptures** is a core competency you'll develop in supervision — and it maps
directly onto these three parts.
