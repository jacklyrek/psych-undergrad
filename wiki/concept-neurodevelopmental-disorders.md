# Neurodevelopmental Disorders: ADHD & Autism Spectrum

The manual's first chapter — first because these conditions **onset in the developmental period**
and are, at core, atypical developmental trajectories rather than acquired episodes. That makes
this the chapter where [[unit05-development]] does the most direct work: every diagnosis here is a
claim that a child's development deviates from age norms *beyond* the (wide) normal range, which
you cannot judge without knowing the norms. The chapter also holds intellectual disability,
specific learning disorder, communication disorders, and tic disorders; counseling practice most
often meets **ADHD** and **autism spectrum disorder**, so they get the depth here.

## ADHD

A persistent, impairing pattern of **inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity**. Criteria
mechanics [S14]:

- **6+ of 9 inattention symptoms** (careless mistakes, can't sustain attention, doesn't seem to
  listen, doesn't finish, disorganized, avoids sustained mental effort, loses things, distractible,
  forgetful) **and/or 6+ of 9 hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms** (fidgets, leaves seat, runs/
  climbs inappropriately — "an internal motor," can't play quietly, talks excessively, blurts,
  can't wait turns, interrupts). **Age 17+: 5+ suffices.**
- **Several symptoms before age 12**, present in **2+ settings** (the two-setting rule screens out
  context problems — a child inattentive *only* at school may have a classroom problem, not ADHD),
  with clear functional interference.
- Three presentations: **predominantly inattentive**, **predominantly hyperactive/impulsive**,
  **combined.** The inattentive presentation — historically underdiagnosed, especially in girls —
  has no behavioral fireworks; it looks like daydreaming and "not living up to potential."

Adult prevalence ~3–6%; a substantial share of childhood cases attenuate by adulthood (roughly
half "grow out of" the full syndrome, especially with treatment), while others persist with the
hyperactivity turned inward (restlessness, disorganization). [S14]

**The differential trap runs both directions:** anxiety and depression impair concentration and can
be misread as ADHD; ADHD's demoralized, scattered adult can be misread as anxious or depressed. The
discriminator is the timeline — ADHD is *lifelong and cross-context* (childhood onset, multiple
settings); an attention problem that started at 30 alongside low mood is not ADHD. [S14]

## Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)

Two symptom pillars, both required, onset in the early developmental period [S15]:

1. **Persistent deficits in social communication and interaction — all three areas:**
   social-emotional reciprocity (back-and-forth conversation, sharing of interests/affect);
   nonverbal communication (eye contact, gesture, facial expression); developing, maintaining, and
   understanding relationships.
2. **Restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities — at least 2 of 4:**
   stereotyped/repetitive movements or speech; insistence on sameness and routines; highly
   restricted, intense fixated interests; sensory hyper- or hypo-reactivity (the DSM-5 addition —
   sound sensitivity, indifference to pain, sensory seeking).

DSM-5's big consolidation: autistic disorder, **Asperger's disorder**, and PDD-NOS were merged into
the single spectrum, graded by **severity levels 1–3** ("requiring support" → "requiring very
substantial support") and specifiers (with/without intellectual or language impairment). [S15]
Prevalence estimates have climbed steeply — CDC 2020: ~**1 in 36** eight-year-olds — a rise that
mixes broadened criteria, better detection, and possibly true increase; it's also Exhibit A in
Frances's diagnostic-inflation argument ([[concept-categorical-vs-dimensional]]). [S15][S21]
Comorbidity is the rule: ~74% have at least one co-occurring condition. [S15]

**Discriminations:** social *anxiety* ([[concept-anxiety-disorders]]) wants connection but fears
judgment, with intact social *understanding*; ASD involves differences in the social-communication
machinery itself, from early development. Schizotypal oddity
([[concept-personality-disorders]]) emerges in adolescence/adulthood; ASD is lifelong.

## In the room

Counselors are often the *first* professional a struggling family or a "why is everything harder
for me than everyone else?" adult consults — your job is usually recognition and referral for
formal evaluation ([[concept-scope-of-practice]]), then support around it. Two stance notes: the
neurodiversity conversation is live (many autistic adults frame autism as difference to be
accommodated, not deficit to be cured — hold the DSM's deficit language *and* the client's
self-understanding without forcing either), and with children, always ask what the *environment*
demands before locating the whole problem in the child — the [[concept-biopsychosocial-model]]
applies to eight-year-olds too.
