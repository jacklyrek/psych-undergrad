# Substance Use Disorders (the DSM view)

This page is deliberately short: the elective module **[[aux-addiction]]** covers addiction at full
depth — models, neuroscience, stages of change, relapse, treatment. What belongs *here* is how
substance use disorders sit inside the DSM-5-TR's architecture, because a few structural facts are
exactly what exams and intakes require.

## One criteria set, eleven criteria, four groups

DSM-5 collapsed DSM-IV's "abuse" vs. "dependence" split into a single **substance use disorder**
per substance class, diagnosed against **11 criteria in four groups** [S16]:

1. **Impaired control** — larger amounts/longer than intended; persistent desire or failed
   attempts to cut down; great time spent obtaining/using/recovering; craving.
2. **Social impairment** — role failure at work/school/home; continued use despite interpersonal
   problems; important activities given up.
3. **Risky use** — use in physically hazardous situations; continued use despite knowing it causes
   physical/psychological harm.
4. **Pharmacological** — tolerance; withdrawal.

Severity is a symptom count — **mild (2–3), moderate (4–5), severe (6+)** — which makes SUD the
manual's most openly *dimensional* diagnosis, a fact worth noticing for
[[concept-categorical-vs-dimensional]]. There's a separate SUD per class (alcohol use disorder,
opioid use disorder, stimulant use disorder…), plus substance-*induced* disorders (intoxication,
withdrawal, and substance-induced mental disorders — always on the differential for
[[concept-mood-disorders | mood]], [[concept-anxiety-disorders | anxiety]], and
[[concept-psychotic-disorders | psychotic]] presentations). [S16]

## The two distinctions that prevent real harm

- **Physical dependence ≠ addiction.** Tolerance and withdrawal that develop under appropriate
  medical care (prescribed opioids, SSRIs, beta-blockers) **do not count** toward an SUD diagnosis.
  A post-surgical patient who needs a taper is not thereby addicted. [S16] Full treatment in
  [[concept-addiction-models]].
- **Behavioral addictions are (almost) not a thing in the DSM.** **Gambling disorder** is the only
  one in the main text; internet gaming disorder sits in Section III for further study. "Addicted
  to X" is colloquial, not diagnostic. [S16]

## Why it matters in this unit

Substance use is the great mimic and the great co-traveler: it can *produce* depression, anxiety,
and psychosis; it co-occurs with nearly every category in this unit (and comorbidity concentrates
severity — [S17]); and missing it reroutes the whole [[concept-treatment-planning | treatment plan]].
The [[concept-intake-interview | intake]] habit is simple:
**always ask about substances, matter-of-factly, whatever the presenting problem.** The stance
material — meeting use without moralizing, the righting reflex, stages of change — is
[[concept-stages-of-change]] and [[concept-treatment-and-recovery]] in the elective.
