# Person-Centered / Humanistic Theory

**Stuck because:** the person's natural drive to grow (the **actualizing tendency**) got blocked —
typically by **conditions of worth** absorbed from others, producing **incongruence** between who
they are and the self-image they feel they must maintain. **Better by:** a genuine relationship that
supplies the **core conditions**, freeing the person to re-access their own capacity for growth. [S3]

This is the theory form of what Unit 1 taught as skills. Where Unit 1 framed empathy, unconditional
positive regard, and congruence as **microskill foundations**, here they are the *whole change
engine.* If you've read [[concept-core-conditions]], you already know this theory's mechanism — this
page situates it as a school. [S3]

## The core ideas

**The actualizing tendency.** Humanistic psychology assumes people are *"inherently motivated toward achieving positive psychological functioning"* — a built-in push toward growth, given the right conditions. [S3] Maslow's hierarchy and Rogers' work share this optimistic premise: humans are fundamentally trustworthy and growth-seeking.

**The client as expert.** The most distinctive commitment. *The client is the expert on their own
life and leads the direction of therapy; the counselor is **non-directive*** — they do **not** give
advice or interpret. [S3][S5] The counselor's job is not to fix but to *create the conditions* under
which the client's own capacity can flourish.

**Conditions of worth & incongruence.** We learn that love/regard is contingent ("you matter *if* you
achieve / behave / please"). We then distort or deny parts of our experience to keep that regard,
creating a gap — **incongruence** — between the real self and the self-concept. Distress is the cost
of that gap. [S3]

**The core conditions** (the therapeutic counter to conditions of worth):
- **Empathy** — understanding from inside the client's frame.
- **Unconditional positive regard** — prizing the person non-contingently.
- **Congruence** — the counselor is genuine, not performing a role.

Full treatment in [[concept-core-conditions]]. [S3]

## How change happens
Not by technique — by *relationship*. When a client experiences accurate empathy, non-contingent
regard, and a genuine other, the conditions of worth lose their grip, the person can re-own denied
experience, incongruence shrinks, and the actualizing tendency does the rest. [S3] Focus is on
**conscious** experience and the **present/future**, not unearthing the past — a deliberate break from
[[theory-psychodynamic]]. [S5]

## What's contested (don't over-learn it)
- **Sufficiency.** Rogers claimed the conditions are necessary *and sufficient*; the mainstream view
  is **necessary but not sufficient** — most clients also need active methods on top. (Detailed in
  [[concept-core-conditions]].) [S3]
- **Vagueness / evidence.** Critics say the principles can be "too vague" to operationalize; efficacy
  evidence is mixed (not inferior to CBT for depression at 6 months, possibly inferior at 12). [S3]
- **Fit.** May be harder going for clients who struggle to talk about themselves or who expect
  direction. [S3]
