# Karl Jaspers (1883–1969)

**Why he matters:** as a young psychiatrist at Heidelberg, Jaspers wrote *General Psychopathology* (1913), which is widely credited with **founding psychopathology as a discipline with its own method**. He introduced careful **phenomenological description** of patients' experiences and the distinction between **understanding** and **explanation**. [S22][S23]

## Life and work, briefly
- Trained in **medicine and psychiatry**, not philosophy. He worked at the Heidelberg psychiatric clinic, then moved into philosophy and became one of the major **existential philosophers** (*Existenzphilosophie*), with concepts like **limit situations** (death, suffering, guilt, struggle). [S22] (The existentialism module covers that side: [[concept-existential-philosophy-roots]].)
- His wife, Gertrud, was Jewish. He was barred from teaching under the Nazis, and the couple survived the war in Heidelberg. Afterward he became a leading voice on German guilt and moved to Basel. (Standard biography.)

## Key ideas (psychiatry)
- **Static understanding** (phenomenology): precise description of what the patient experiences, from their own account and the clinician's empathic re-presentation. [S22]
- **Genetic understanding vs. causal explanation** (*Verstehen* vs. *Erklären*), taken from Dilthey. [S22][S23] See [[concept-understanding-vs-explanation]].
- **Form vs. content** of symptoms. [S24]
- **Primary delusion** as un-understandable, not derivable from prior experience. [S24][S25]

## Caveats
- How much Jaspers took from **Husserl** is debated. His phenomenology was **empirical and descriptive**, not Husserl's eidetic method. [S22]
- The **understandability criterion** is criticized for placing psychotic experience beyond empathy. [S24]
