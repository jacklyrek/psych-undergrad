# Erikson's Eight Psychosocial Stages

**The idea:** development is not finished in childhood, and it is not driven mainly by sexuality.
Erik Erikson took Freud's psychosexual stages ([[theory-psychodynamic]]) and did two things to them:
he made the engine **social** (each stage is a tension between the person and their social world,
not a zone of the body), and he extended it **across the whole lifespan**, adding adult and old-age
stages Freud never had. [S1] The result is a map of the **central task the person is wrestling with
at each phase of life** — which makes it, clinically, a "where is this person stuck?" lens.

## The structure

Each stage is a **psychosocial crisis**: a tension between a positive (**syntonic**) and a negative
(**dystonic**) pole. Healthy development is *not* getting 100% of the positive and none of the
negative — some mistrust, some doubt, some guilt is adaptive. It's landing on a **favorable ratio**,
which yields a lasting **ego virtue / basic strength**. [S1] Two features matter clinically:

- The stages are **epigenetic** — they unfold in a set sequence, each building on the last, each
  becoming salient at its life phase.
- Resolution is **not required to advance**, and an unresolved crisis can be **reworked later.** [S1]
  You don't fail out of "trust vs. mistrust" forever; a later secure relationship (or therapy) can
  revisit it. This is Erikson's version of the whole unit's *risk, not destiny*.

## The eight stages [S1]

| # | Life phase | Crisis (syntonic vs. dystonic) | Virtue | The task, in a line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Infancy (0–~1.5) | **Trust vs. Mistrust** | **Hope** | Is the world reliable? (the psychosocial face of [[theory-attachment]]) |
| 2 | Early childhood (~1.5–3) | **Autonomy vs. Shame & Doubt** | **Will** | Can I do things myself? (toilet training, "no," the will) |
| 3 | Play age (~3–6) | **Initiative vs. Guilt** | **Purpose** | Can I plan and start things without feeling bad for wanting to? |
| 4 | School age (~6–12) | **Industry vs. Inferiority** | **Competence** | Can I make things and be good at them, among peers? |
| 5 | Adolescence (~12–18) | **Identity vs. Role Confusion** | **Fidelity** | Who am I, and what do I believe? |
| 6 | Young adulthood (~18–40) | **Intimacy vs. Isolation** | **Love** | Can I fuse my identity with another's without losing it? |
| 7 | Adulthood (~40–65) | **Generativity vs. Stagnation** | **Care** | Am I contributing to the next generation / something beyond myself? |
| 8 | Old age (65+) | **Integrity vs. Despair** | **Wisdom** | Looking back, was my life meaningful? |

(Joan Erikson later sketched a tentative **ninth stage** for very old age, revisiting all eight under
the conditions of frailty — worth knowing exists, not usually tested. [S1])

## The two most-confused pairs (the exam traps)

- **Initiative (3) vs. Industry (4).** *Initiative* is the drive to **start** and imagine ("let's
  build a rocket ship!"); its failure is guilt over wanting/doing. *Industry* is the drive to
  **complete and be competent** at real tasks, measured against peers; its failure is inferiority.
  Initiative = the *impulse to begin*; industry = the *capacity to finish and be good at it.*
- **Identity (5) vs. Intimacy (6).** *Identity* is figuring out **who you are** on your own;
  *intimacy* is the capacity to **merge that self with another** without dissolving it. Erikson's
  order is deliberate and clinical: **you need a self before you can share one** — someone who skips
  identity work often does "intimacy" as enmeshment or serial fusing, losing themselves in each
  partner.

## Using it clinically

Erikson is a **formulation heuristic**, not a test you administer:

- **Locate the live crisis.** A 19-year-old churning over major, values, and who to be is doing
  *normal* identity work — not necessarily pathology (see [[unit05-development]] on typical vs.
  clinical). A 45-year-old in a "what has my life been for?" crisis is at generativity/stagnation, not
  simply "depressed."
- **Spot carried-forward crises.** A client who cannot trust the alliance may be reworking stage 1;
  a client paralyzed by shame around autonomy may be reworking stage 2. The framework tells you
  *which developmental task the presenting problem echoes.*
- **Reframe life-stage distress as a task, not a defect.** "Empty nest," retirement, midlife, the
  late-life life review — Erikson lets you name these as developmental work rather than disorders.

## What's contested (don't over-learn it)

- **It's a heuristic, not measured fact.** The theory is hard to falsify, the ages are approximate,
  and the "crises" aren't cleanly separable or sequential in real lives. [S1]
- **It's culturally and historically situated.** An extended *adolescence* devoted to *identity
  exploration* is largely a modern, Western, industrialized arrangement; "identity," "autonomy," and
  "generativity" carry individualist assumptions that don't travel unchanged — connect this to
  [[unit04-multicultural]] (worldview, the individualist default). [S1]
- **The virtues are aspirational, not guaranteed** — the point is a favorable *ratio*, and later
  stages can rework earlier ones. Don't read the table as a pass/fail ladder.
