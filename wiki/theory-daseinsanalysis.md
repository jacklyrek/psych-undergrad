# Daseinsanalysis

**Stuck because:** the person's **way of being-in-the-world has narrowed or rigidified**. Only a few possibilities still show up as open. Their surroundings, their relations with others, and their relation to themselves are all lived through one constricted "world-design." **Better by:** the therapist and patient carefully *seeing together* how the patient's world is structured, without reducing it to hidden causes, so that more of the patient's own possibilities can open up. [S14][S15]

Daseinsanalysis (German: "analysis of Dasein," i.e., of human existence; [[concept-being-in-the-world]]) was the first systematic attempt to build psychiatry and psychotherapy on Heidegger's philosophy. It is the school Peterson describes in his personality lectures as the phenomenologists who thought "your environment is part of you." [S47] There are **two founders with two different versions**, and they are easy to blur.

## The two founders

| | **Ludwig Binswanger** (1881–1966) | **Medard Boss** (1903–1990) |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Swiss psychiatrist; director of the Bellevue sanatorium, Kreuzlingen | Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst, Zurich |
| Called it | *Daseinsanalyse* / "existential analysis" (as a psychiatric-anthropological research method) | *Daseinsanalysis* as a **therapy** |
| Relation to Freud | friend of Freud's for decades; tried to put psychoanalysis on a phenomenological footing | trained as an analyst; kept the analytic *setting* but dropped its causal-energetic theory |
| Relation to Heidegger | read *Being and Time* creatively and was **rejected by Heidegger** for confusing its ontological analysis with an everyday, ontic psychology | worked with Heidegger directly; hosted the **Zollikon Seminars** (1959–1969) at his home, where Heidegger taught psychiatrists |
| Signature | world-design; **Umwelt / Mitwelt / Eigenwelt**; long case studies (Ellen West) | phenomenological **dream** work; the "existentials" (spatiality, temporality, bodyhood, being-with, mood, mortality) |

**The split.** After a 1957 conference in Madrid, Boss published a critique of Freud, Jung, **and Binswanger**, and the two broke. Binswanger felt betrayed by both Boss and Heidegger and turned back toward Husserl. [S17] So "Daseinsanalysis" today mostly means the **Boss** lineage, institutionalized in Zurich by Gion Condrau, with an International Federation founded in 1990. [S14]

## Core ideas

**1. The three "worlds" (Binswanger, popularized by May).** Every human existence is lived simultaneously in three regions of world: [S15]

| World | Literally | What it is | Example of disturbance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Umwelt** | "around-world" | the natural and physical environment: body, biology, surroundings, needs, drives | a body experienced only as an enemy; the physical world as threatening |
| **Mitwelt** | "with-world" | the world of relationships with others: community, love, conflict | others experienced only as judges or rivals |
| **Eigenwelt** | "own-world" | one's relationship to oneself: self-awareness, self-relatedness | no inner place to stand; self experienced as empty or alien |

These are **not three separate places**. They are three aspects of one being-in-the-world, always present together. The clinical question is how they are lived, and which are collapsed or overgrown. [S15] (The British existential school's **four** dimensions, van Deurzen's physical / social / personal / spiritual, extend this triad; see the existentialism module, [[theory-existential-therapy-schools]].)

**2. World-design (*Weltentwurf*).** Binswanger's term for the overall structure through which a person's world is disclosed: the basic "shape" of their existence, within which all their experiences get their meaning. In pathology the world-design constricts. Everything is lived under one dominant theme (in Ellen West's case, a split between a light, airy **ethereal world** she longed for and a dark, heavy **tomb-world** of body, earth, and eating that she felt trapped in). [S15] See [[study-ellen-west]].

**3. No hidden mechanism behind the phenomenon.** Unlike psychoanalysis, Daseinsanalysis does **not** treat symptoms as disguised expressions of hidden drives or energies. It uses phenomenological *seeing* to illuminate what a symptom **means** as a way of being-in-the-world. [S14]

**4. Dreams (Boss).** Boss rejected Freud's decoding of latent content behind the manifest dream. The dream is to be taken **as it shows itself**, as a way the dreamer exists in that moment, with the openness or constriction that dream-world reveals. The therapeutic question is roughly: *what does this dream show about how you are open, or closed, to the world, and how might that be true waking too?* [S14][S18]

**5. The relationship.** The therapeutic relationship is a real encounter in which therapist and patient together disclose the patient's existence. It is not a screen for projection. [S14]

## How it looks in practice
- The classical Boss format kept a **psychoanalytic frame** (frequent sessions, couch possible), with the interpretation style replaced by phenomenological exploration. [S14]
- Training (per the federation) is long: **roughly 7 years** in total, including personal analysis and supervised practice. [S14]
- For the counseling student, the practical legacy is less the formal method than two habits: (a) **map the client's three worlds** (surroundings and body, others, self) and notice which are constricted; (b) **treat symptoms and dreams as meaningful ways of being**, not as noise from an inner machine. (Interpretive bridge.)

## What's contested (don't over-learn it)
- **Thin outcome evidence.** There is little controlled outcome research on Daseinsanalysis specifically. The existential therapies as a group have a modest and uneven evidence base (see the existentialism module's [[study-existential-therapy-outcomes]]). Treat it as a **lens**, not an evidence-based treatment.
- **Heidegger rejected Binswanger's version**, and there is a long scholarly debate about whether that was a real disagreement or a misunderstanding. [S17]
- **Boss's dream method** was criticized, notably by Eugene Gendlin, for claiming to "let the dream speak" while importing Heideggerian concepts in place of Freudian ones. [S18]
- **The Ellen West case** (below) is both the school's most famous exhibit and its most serious ethical indictment. [S19][S20]
- **Heidegger's Nazism** hangs over any school built this directly on him ([[concept-being-in-the-world]]). [S3]
