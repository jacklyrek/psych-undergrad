# Screening & Outcome-Monitoring Tools (PHQ-9, GAD-7, and others)

A **screening tool** is a brief, standardized self-report that turns a fuzzy clinical impression into a **number you can compare** — across clients, and across weeks for the *same* client. Two of them, the **PHQ-9** and the **GAD-7**, are so common in real practice that you'll administer them constantly, so learn them cold. But the load-bearing idea comes first, because it's the one that's easy to get wrong: **a screen is not a diagnosis.** It flags a *domain of distress* and grades its *severity*; the diagnosis is still made by the clinical interview ([[concept-intake-interview]]) and the criteria ([[unit06-psychopathology]]).

## The PHQ-9 (depression)

The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 — Kroenke, Spitzer & Williams (2001) [S2]:

- **9 items**, one for **each of the nine DSM major-depression symptoms**, each scored **0–3** ("not at all" → "nearly every day") over the **last two weeks** → **total 0–27.** (Because the nine items *are* the nine criteria, the PHQ-9 doubles as a criteria refresher — [[concept-mood-disorders]].)
- **Severity anchors: 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 = mild / moderate / moderately severe / severe** (0–4 minimal).
- **Cut-point ≥10**: sensitivity **88%**, specificity **88%** for major depression [S2]. Scoring ≥10 makes a depression diagnosis 7–14× more likely; ≤4 drops it below ~1-in-25 [S6].
- **Reliability:** Cronbach's α ≈ **0.86–0.89** (good internal consistency) [S2].
- **Item 9 is a suicide-risk trip-wire** — "thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself." *Any* non-zero answer obligates a direct, in-person risk follow-up; never let a self-report scale substitute for the conversation ([[concept-suicide-risk-assessment]]).
- **Free and public-domain**, in 30+ languages [S6] — a real reason it's everywhere.
- **PHQ-2** = the first two items (mood + anhedonia) as an **ultra-brief pre-screen**; a positive PHQ-2 (≥3) prompts the full PHQ-9.

## The GAD-7 (anxiety)

The Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 — Spitzer, Kroenke, Williams & Löwe (2006) [S3]:

- **7 items**, each **0–3** over the **last two weeks** → **total 0–21.**
- **Cut-points: 5 / 10 / 15 = mild / moderate / severe.**
- **Cut-point ≥10**: sensitivity **89%**, specificity **82%** for GAD [S3]. Internal consistency α ≈ **0.83–0.92** across samples [S3][S10].
- **It is not GAD-specific.** Though built for GAD, it also screens positive for **panic disorder** (~74%), **social anxiety disorder** (~72%), and **PTSD** (~66%) [S3] — the clean illustration that a positive screen names a *broad domain of distress*, not a diagnosis. A high GAD-7 sends you to the interview to find out *which* anxiety, not to write "GAD." (**GAD-2** = the first two items as the brief pre-screen.)

## Screen once — then keep measuring (measurement-based care)

The highest-value use of these tools isn't the one-time intake flag; it's **repeated administration to track change.** **Measurement-based care (MBC)** = giving a validated scale (PHQ-9, GAD-7, …) at *each visit* and using the score to steer treatment [S12]. Why it matters:

- It **catches non-response and deterioration earlier** than clinical impression — clinicians relying on unaided judgment routinely fail to notice a client is worsening, and the benefit of MBC is **strongest precisely for the patients who would otherwise quietly not improve** [S12].
- It closes the loop with the plan: the same instrument that flagged the problem now **defines a measurable goal** ("PHQ-9 from 18 to <10 in 12 weeks") and shows whether you're getting there ([[concept-treatment-planning]]). This is the outcome-monitoring thread [[concept-single-case-design | Unit 12]] picks up.

## The trap: a number is a flag, not a verdict

Screens are **deliberately tuned toward high sensitivity** — better to over-flag and assess than to miss a case ([[concept-reliability-validity]]) — which *guarantees* false positives. So:

- **Never diagnose from a score.** A PHQ-9 of 16 is a reason to interview carefully, not a diagnosis of MDD. The score can't tell grief from depression, or a bad week from an episode ([[concept-mood-disorders]]).
- **Cut-points are population-dependent, not laws.** ≥10 is the standard flag, but ≥8 is optimal in some psychiatric samples [S10] — a calibrated setting, not a threshold of truth.
- **Read the score in context.** Self-report is filtered through culture, language, literacy, and motive-to-present ([[unit04-multicultural]]); a validity coefficient earned in one population may not travel ([[concept-reliability-validity]]).
- **Don't let the form run the session.** MBC serves the work; a client is a person, not a trend line — the same "measure, don't be captured by the measure" discipline the whole unit teaches.
