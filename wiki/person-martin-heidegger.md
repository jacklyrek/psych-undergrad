# Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)

**Why he matters:** Heidegger turned phenomenology from the study of **consciousness** into the study of **existence**: of *Dasein* as **being-in-the-world**. *Being and Time* (1927) is the direct source of Daseinsanalysis, of much existential therapy, of hermeneutic research methods, and of the enactive strand of cognitive science. [S1][S3]

## Life and work, briefly
- Husserl's assistant at Freiburg, and later his successor there. *Being and Time* was dedicated to Husserl, but it broke decisively with him by rejecting the priority of bracketed consciousness. [S1][S3]
- Core concepts: Dasein; being-in-the-world; ready-to-hand vs. present-at-hand; thrownness; mood (*Befindlichkeit*); understanding as projection; *das Man*; care; anxiety vs. fear; being-toward-death; authenticity. [S3] Full treatment: [[concept-being-in-the-world]], [[concept-mood-attunement]].
- **Zollikon Seminars (1959–1969):** Heidegger taught psychiatrists at Medard Boss's home near Zurich. This was the only sustained engagement of a major philosopher with clinicians on those terms, and the foundation of Boss's Daseinsanalysis. He rejected Binswanger's earlier appropriation of his work. [S14][S16][S17] See [[theory-daseinsanalysis]].

## The Nazism problem (do not skip this)
Heidegger **joined the Nazi party in 1933** and served as **rector of Freiburg University** that year. His posthumously published **Black Notebooks** contain antisemitic passages. The SEP entry treats these issues directly and reports that scholarly opinion ranges from "the philosophy is separable from the politics" to "they are essentially entangled." [S3]

What to do with that as a student:
- You can use a concept like "being-in-the-world" without endorsing its author. Many clinicians do.
- Don't present him as a neutral sage. Some scholars and clinicians decline to build on him for these reasons, and that is a defensible position.
- His treatment of Husserl, who was of Jewish origin and lost his university privileges under the regime, is part of the same record. [S2][S3]

## How he differs
- **vs. Husserl:** existence before consciousness; interpretation all the way down (no complete bracketing). [S1]
- **vs. Sartre and the existentialists:** Heidegger rejected the label "existentialist." His project was the *question of being*, not a philosophy of individual freedom. (The existentialism module covers that lineage: [[concept-existential-philosophy-roots]].)
