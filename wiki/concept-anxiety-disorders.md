# Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety disorders are the **most common category of mental disorder** — 12-month prevalence ~18% of U.S. adults, with up to ~20% experiencing one in a given year. [S17][S7] Fear and worry are adaptive; the disorders begin where the alarm is excessive, persistent, hard to control, and impairing. The chapter's internal logic is a single question: **what is the fear about, and when does it fire?** Answer that, and the differential mostly solves itself.

Note what *isn't* here anymore: DSM-5 moved OCD to its own chapter ([[concept-ocd]]) and PTSD to the trauma- and stressor-related chapter ([[concept-trauma-stressor-disorders]]). Exams love this fact; more importantly, it reminds you that obsessional loops and trauma responses are not just "anxiety." [S12][S9]

## Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD)

One of the syllabus's "big three." **Excessive anxiety and worry, more days than not, for ≥6 months, about multiple domains** (work, health, family, finances), which the person finds **difficult to control**, with **3+ of 6** associated symptoms: restlessness/keyed-up feeling, easy fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance — plus distress/impairment and no substance/medical explanation (rule out hyperthyroidism, caffeine, stimulants). [S7] The signature is *free-floating, topic-hopping worry about ordinary life* — the worry migrates; contrast the single circumscribed fears below. Women ~2× men; the course is chronic and relapsing. [S7]

## Panic attacks vs. panic disorder

A **panic attack** is "an abrupt surge of intense fear or discomfort" that **peaks within minutes**, with **4+ of 13** symptoms: palpitations, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, choking sensation, chest pain, nausea, dizziness, chills/heat, paresthesias, derealization/depersonalization, fear of losing control or "going crazy," fear of dying. [S8] Attacks are a *symptom*, not a diagnosis — they occur across many disorders (and in medical conditions; cardiac, thyroid, and vestibular mimics must be excluded).

**Panic disorder** requires **recurrent *unexpected* attacks** — out of a clear blue sky — plus **≥1 month** of worry about further attacks and/or maladaptive behavior change (avoiding exercise, ERs, being alone). [S8] The discrimination hinge is **expected vs. unexpected**: panic at the podium is social anxiety disorder's panic; panic at the sight of the dog is the phobia's; panic from nowhere, repeatedly, plus fear-of-the-fear, is panic disorder. The fear here is *of the attack itself* — "fear of fear."

**Agoraphobia** — its historical companion, now a separate diagnosis — is fear/avoidance of situations where escape might be difficult or help unavailable if panic-like symptoms strike (public transport, open/enclosed spaces, crowds, being out alone): at root, fear of *being trapped away from safety*.

## The circumscribed fears

- **Specific phobia:** marked, immediate fear of a particular object/situation (animals, heights, blood-injection-injury, flying), actively avoided, ≥6 months, out of proportion to actual danger. Lifetime prevalence ~13% — among the most common disorders anywhere in the manual. [S1]
- **Social anxiety disorder:** fear of social situations where one may be **scrutinized and negatively evaluated** — humiliation, embarrassment, rejection (~12% lifetime). The fear is about *the audience*, not the performance space; contrast agoraphobia's fear of being trapped. [S1]
- **Separation anxiety disorder:** developmentally excessive fear about separation from attachment figures — no longer a childhood-only diagnosis. The word *developmentally* is load-bearing: separation protest is **normal** in toddlers ([[theory-attachment]]); the diagnosis exists only where the fear outruns the developmental stage ([[unit05-development]]'s typical-vs-clinical rule).

## A worked discrimination table

| Disorder | The fear, in one phrase | Trigger pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GAD | "everything could go wrong" | diffuse, migrating, chronic |
| Panic disorder | "the attack itself" | unexpected, then anticipatory |
| Agoraphobia | "I'll be trapped, no help" | situations hard to escape |
| Social anxiety | "they'll judge me" | scrutiny/evaluation |
| Specific phobia | "that thing" | one circumscribed cue |
| Separation anxiety | "losing my person" | separation from attachment figure |

## In the room

Anxious clients frequently arrive asking you to help them *stop feeling anxious* — and reassurance is the trap. Reassurance and avoidance are the two behaviors that *maintain* anxiety disorders (each relief teaches the alarm it was right to fire), which is why the evidence-based treatments (Unit 9: CBT, exposure) run through the fear rather than around it. Your microskills matter first: an anxious client who feels rushed will perform calm and change nothing. Also normalize the body: many panic clients have been to the ER convinced they were dying — the 2-minute physiology explanation is itself therapeutic.
