# Ludwig Binswanger & Medard Boss

Both were Swiss psychiatrists with psychoanalytic roots, and both tried to rebuild psychiatry on Heidegger. They ended up with **two different "Daseinsanalyses"** and a falling-out. Knowing who did what keeps the concepts straight. [S14][S17]

## Ludwig Binswanger (1881–1966): the psychiatric anthropologist
- Director of the family's **Bellevue sanatorium** in **Kreuzlingen**, and a **lifelong friend of Freud's** despite theoretical disagreement. [S17]
- Read Heidegger's *Being and Time* as a basis for understanding each patient's **world-design** (*Weltentwurf*). Developed the triad **Umwelt / Mitwelt / Eigenwelt**, which Rollo May's *Existence* (1958) made famous in English. [S15]
- Wrote long, literary case studies. The best known is **Ellen West**, now also the most criticized ([[study-ellen-west]]). [S15][S19][S20]
- **Heidegger rejected his reading** as a misunderstanding that collapsed the ontological structure of existence into everyday psychology. After the break with Boss, Binswanger returned to Husserl. [S17]

## Medard Boss (1903–1990): the therapist
- Zurich psychiatrist who had been in analysis with Freud and trained with Jung's circle; kept the psychoanalytic **setting** but dropped the **drive/energy theory**. [S14][S17]
- Became Heidegger's clinical collaborator. He hosted the **Zollikon Seminars** (1959–1969) and edited them for publication. [S14][S16]
- Known for **phenomenological dream work**: take the dream as it shows itself, as the dreamer's mode of being-in-the-world, rather than decoding it (*The Analysis of Dreams*, 1958). [S18] Gendlin criticized this. [S18]
- Published a critique of Freud, Jung, and Binswanger after a **1957** Madrid conference; the split followed. [S17]
