# Schizophrenia Spectrum & Other Psychotic Disorders

Psychosis means a break with consensual reality — and this chapter organizes its diagnoses along two axes you can hold in one hand: **which symptoms** (out of five domains) and **how long**. Learn the five domains and the duration ladder and the whole spectrum becomes readable.

## The five symptom domains

Schizophrenia's Criterion A lists five, of which **two or more** must be present for a significant portion of one month — and at least one must be from the first three [S10]:

1. **Delusions** — fixed false beliefs held against contrary evidence (persecutory, referential, grandiose, somatic, erotomanic).
2. **Hallucinations** — perceptions without an external stimulus; auditory ("hearing voices") is most common in schizophrenia.
3. **Disorganized speech** — derailment, incoherence, "word salad" (a *thought* disorder made audible).
4. **Grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior.**
5. **Negative symptoms** — diminished emotional expression, avolition (loss of motivated, self-initiated activity), alogia, anhedonia, asociality.

The first four are **positive symptoms** — additions to experience; the fifth cluster is **negative** — subtractions from it. The distinction predicts treatment response: antipsychotics handle positive symptoms far better than negative ones, and negative symptoms carry much of the long-term disability. [S10] (Delusion vs. hallucination is the beginner discrimination: a belief vs. a perception. And a PTSD flashback is neither — see [[concept-trauma-stressor-disorders]].)

## The duration ladder

Same symptom pool, different clocks — this is the spectrum's core discrimination set [S10]:

| Diagnosis | Duration | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brief psychotic disorder** | 1 day – <1 month | full return to prior functioning; often stress-precipitated |
| **Schizophreniform disorder** | 1 – <6 months | schizophrenia's picture, shorter clock; no functional-decline requirement |
| **Schizophrenia** | **≥6 months** of continuous signs, incl. **≥1 month** active-phase | with functional decline; prodromal/residual phases count toward the 6 |
| **Delusional disorder** | ≥1 month of delusions **only** | functioning largely intact; none of the other four domains prominent |

**Schizoaffective disorder** sits off the ladder: a major mood episode (depressive or manic) concurrent with active-phase psychotic symptoms, **plus ≥2 weeks of delusions/hallucinations *without* a mood episode** — that mood-free psychosis window is what separates it from a [[concept-mood-disorders | mood disorder]] with psychotic features (where psychosis appears *only* inside mood episodes). [S10]

## The facts that shape clinical care

Lifetime risk ~1%, strikingly stable across populations; men are diagnosed ~1.7× as often, earlier (early twenties vs. late twenties), and with more negative symptoms. Onset in adolescence/early adulthood, often after a prodrome of withdrawal and odd ideas. Course is heterogeneous but sobering: complete recovery in ~13.5%; life expectancy runs ~15 years short, and **suicide is the most common cause of premature death** — psychosis and [[concept-suicide-risk-assessment | Unit 8's risk work]] overlap heavily. [S10]

The reigning (and incomplete) mechanistic story is the **dopamine hypothesis**: mesolimbic dysregulation decouples dopamine signaling from genuinely salient events, producing "misattribution of importance to irrelevant objects or events" — *aberrant salience*, a compact way to understand how referential delusions feel from inside (everything seems to mean something). [S10] [[unit11-neuroscience | Unit 11]] picks up the neurobiology (the revised [[concept-neuron-neurotransmission | dopamine hypothesis]]) and [[concept-psychotropic-classes | antipsychotics]] in full.

## In the room

Counselors rarely treat acute psychosis alone — this is coordinated-care territory ([[concept-helping-professions-compared]]) and often a scope boundary ([[concept-scope-of-practice]]). But you *will* sit with people who hear voices, hold delusions, or are recovering from an episode, so the stance matters: **don't argue with a delusion, and don't endorse it.** Arguing entrenches; colluding betrays. Reflect the *experience* ("that sounds frightening — you feel watched everywhere") without ruling on its content. And person-first language is at its most consequential here: "a person with schizophrenia," never "a schizophrenic" — this diagnosis carries the field's heaviest stigma load. [S1]

Culture check before pathology check: hearing a deceased relative's voice during grief is normative in many cultures, and "paranoid" wariness can be a rational learning from surveillance or racism — run the [[concept-dsm-structure | cultural formulation]] before the label. [S23]
