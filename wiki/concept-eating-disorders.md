# Feeding & Eating Disorders

Three disorders carry this chapter's clinical weight, and they sort on two questions: **is body
weight significantly low?** and **is there compensation after binges?** Hold those two hinges and
the discriminations fall out. What must never fall out of view: **anorexia nervosa is among the
deadliest diagnoses in the entire manual** — this chapter is medical, not cosmetic.

## Anorexia nervosa (AN)

Three criteria, all required [S13]:

1. **Restriction of energy intake leading to significantly low body weight** (for age, sex,
   development).
2. **Intense fear of gaining weight or becoming fat**, or persistent behavior that prevents weight
   gain — a fear that, tellingly, *intensifies as weight falls*.
3. **Disturbance in how body weight/shape is experienced**, undue influence of weight/shape on
   self-worth, or persistent failure to recognize the seriousness of the low weight.

Two subtypes: **restricting** (dieting, fasting, exercise only) and **binge-eating/purging** (with
binge or purge behavior — *still AN, not bulimia, because weight is low*). Lifetime prevalence
0.3–1%, mostly female, onset in late adolescence/early adulthood. [S13]

The mortality warning deserves its own line: all-cause mortality is sharply elevated — medical
complications (cardiac, electrolyte) plus **suicide, which accounts for roughly a quarter of AN
deaths**. [S13] Denial is close to criterial (criterion 3): the disorder is typically
**ego-syntonic** — the client may experience the restriction as achievement and the helpers as the
threat, which is why motivation work precedes everything and why outpatient counseling always runs
alongside medical monitoring ([[concept-scope-of-practice]]; coordinated care).

## Bulimia nervosa (BN)

**Recurrent binge eating** — a discrete period of objectively large intake *with a sense of loss of
control* — **plus recurrent inappropriate compensatory behavior** (self-induced vomiting,
laxatives/diuretics, fasting, driven exercise), both at least weekly for 3 months, with self-worth
unduly tied to shape/weight. **The weight criterion is the AN/BN line: in BN, weight is normal or
above.** [S13] Same behaviors at low weight → AN binge-purge subtype. Medical risks concentrate
around purging (electrolyte disturbance, esophageal damage, dental erosion). Unlike AN, BN is
usually **ego-dystonic** — shame-soaked and hidden, often for years; asking directly and without
disgust is the whole screening skill.

## Binge-eating disorder (BED)

Recurrent binges with loss of control and marked distress — eating rapidly, until uncomfortably
full, without hunger, alone out of embarrassment, followed by disgust/guilt — **without
compensatory behavior** (that absence is the BN line). At least weekly for 3 months. New as an
official diagnosis in DSM-5, and the **most common eating disorder in the U.S.**; more
gender-balanced than AN/BN.

## The rest of the chapter, briefly

**ARFID** (avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder): restriction with nutritional consequences
but **no body-image disturbance** — sensory aversion, fear of choking, low interest (common
alongside [[concept-neurodevelopmental-disorders | autism]]); it is what separates "extreme picky
eating with impairment" from AN. Plus pica and rumination disorder.

| | Low weight? | Binges? | Compensation? | Body-image driver? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AN | **yes** | maybe | maybe | yes |
| BN | no | yes | **yes** | yes |
| BED | no | yes | **no** | distress, not compensation |
| ARFID | maybe | no | no | **no** |

## In the room

Distinguish the *disorder* from the *culture that grows it*: dieting, "clean eating," and body talk
are normative — which both camouflages early eating disorders and means your own casual comments
about food/weight/appearance are clinical acts in this population. Screen behaviorally ("Do you
ever eat in a way that feels out of control?" "What happens after?") rather than by appearance —
**most people with eating disorders are not visibly underweight** (BN and BED by definition), and
compliments on weight loss can reinforce restriction. [[concept-ocd | Body dysmorphic disorder]]
is the near neighbor: appearance obsession without the eating pathology.
